3

Joy and Bewilderment

“Where is that Machchasaami?”

At Malaikkozhundha Gounder's question, which thundered like a peal of thunder, Perumayi Ammal and Thamarai Naachchi stood speechless without giving any answer.

“Here I am, Maama!” Nelliyangodan said, emerging from behind the silk curtain hanging there. There was no fear on his face. Perhaps it was the foolish courage of knowing that his aunt and his beloved Thamarai were nearby!

“It was not enough merely to put you in the shed! I should have killed you. Because an auspicious event is taking place in the house, I stopped with only this much—and that has turned out to be my mistake!” he roared, lunging forward, seizing Nelliyangodan by the hair and shaking him.

“Let him go!” Perumayi Ammal said, grabbing his hand and pulling it away. “Appa! Kill me first, and then kill him!” Thamarai cried, stepping between them.

“Are the women running the kingdom in this house? Because of what the two of you have done, tomorrow not only this village but all the neighbouring countries will spit at me in contempt!” Malaikkozhundha Gounder burst out in fury.

“What have we done? You listened to Chellaatha Gounder, concluded that this boy had gone somewhere and died, and arranged

<!-- source boundary: scan 34 → scan 35 -->

Thamarai's marriage. Now he is alive. Chellaatha Gounder made all his possessions his own, beat and kicked him, made him herd goats and cattle, and the boy ran away fearing that if he stayed his very life would be in danger. Only now has he returned. And you leap at him—is that just? You tell me yourself!” Perumayi Ammal asked, tears flowing from her eyes. For a moment Malaikkozhundha Gounder stood motionless. Then he began to speak:

“All right, he has returned. But has he not returned as a madman whose mind is unsound?”

Hearing this, Nelliyangodan smiled gently, called out “Maama!” with affection overflowing, and said:

“If a man does not know deceit or guile, does that make him mad? Even if a snake comes to bite me, instead of taking a stick to strike it I would fold my hands and ask, ‘Go away; do nothing to me!’ If the snake still came saying it would certainly bite, I would accept even that. Is that what is called madness, Maama?” he asked with feeling.

“Did you see? Did you see what a good heart he has? Was it not an outrage to lock such a boy in that shed and torment him?”

Perumayi Ammal clasped her husband's feet and melted like wax.

“All right; what happened has happened. What can be done now? We can attend to what must be done for the wedding in the morning,” Malaikkozhundha Gounder said. Perumayi again asked in a pleading voice, “For him and our daughter, isn't it?”

Suppressing his anger, he shook his wife's shoulder and asked sarcastically, “We do not have another daughter besides Thamarai, do we?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 35 → scan 36 -->

“Appa! You betrothed me to Aththaan when I was still a child. I too have kept him in my heart and worshipped him there. Even when you decided to marry me to Maanthiyappan, I kept expecting that my Aththaan would somehow return. He has returned just as I thought he would. Do not refuse any longer; conduct our wedding for us, Appa!” There was anguish in Thamarai's words.

“Maama! I too will forget all the cruelty you inflicted and marry her!” Nelliyangodan said in the manner of “let us forget and forgive.”

“Oh! So, out of mercy, as though saying let bygones be bygones, you are graciously agreeing to marry my daughter?” the Gounder of Maniyangurichi growled.

“Your son-in-law always talks playfully like this. Why take it seriously? All right, leave that aside! Just say that you consent to conduct the wedding in the morning according to Thamarai Naachchi's wish!” Perumayi Ammal pleaded.

“Yes, Maama! If you will only say now that you have agreed, Thamarai and I will lie tonight in separate rooms, on separate beds, and dream sweet dreams all night! In that dream all the clouds in the sky will float like boats! The rainbow will come as an ornamented boat. Thamarai will recline gracefully in it, wearing all the stars strung together as jewels. I, prince of the lunar realm, will suddenly leap from there into that decorated boat. The moment she sees me, Thamarai will run to me... run to me...”

Before Nelliyangodan could finish his full description, his aunt interrupted and cleverly stopped him, saying he could talk about all this with Thamarai after the wedding was over.

<!-- source boundary: scan 36 → scan 37 -->

Malaikkozhundha Gounder, who had burst through the door like a volcano spewing fire, understood Perumayi Ammal's affection born of kinship for Nelliyangodan and the loving heart of Thamarai Naachchi, who said she had been waiting to marry him. He therefore tried to use a different strategy to change their minds.

“Chellaatha Gounder is many times more eminent and prosperous than we are. He has influence and men at his command equal to a minor kingdom. Is it good for us to make an enemy of him?”

The moment he raised this question, Nelliyangodan made his face sorrowful and said, “What Maama says is right too! If Chellaatha Gounder is Yama, his son Maanthiyappan is a servant of Yama! If we seek the enmity of both, it would be no surprise if danger came to Maniyangurichi afterward. Think carefully before deciding.” Distressed that her Aththaan's speech was drifting into foolishness, Thamarai began sobbing: “Appa! Do you at least now understand how good Aththaan is? How could I betray such a noble man?”

“All that is true, child; do not forget that the honour of our Maniyangurichi is also involved in this!”

“Is it right, Appa, to neglect your daughter's life for the sake of your problem of honour?”

Now Perumayi Ammal intervened calmly.

“Could you not tell Chellaatha Gounder the true facts, pacify him, and ask him to remain here and conduct this wedding too?”

On hearing his wife's suggestion, Malaikkozhundha Gounder's face tightened irritably.

<!-- source boundary: scan 37 → scan 38 -->

Muttering something, he paced back and forth inside the room. Then, looking at his wife and daughter, he began explaining the difficult position he was in.

“Look here, Perumayi! Thamarai! Maanthiyappan was formally fixed as the groom, and the *arumaip periyor* have already taken the ceremonial trays, circled water around them, and handed them to married women. The gold for the *muppari thaali* has been given, and the goldsmith has already made it and sent it. The ritual of boiling and drying paddy has been completed in the wedding house. Milk was poured and worship offered to a milk-bearing tree; a branch was cut, tied in three bundles and placed to dry on the roof of the wedding pavilion. The two households have exchanged two baskets of salt. Earth from an anthill has been taken according to custom to make, plaster and smooth the platform in the wedding chamber. Elders from the twenty-four *naadu* have been invited to give blessings, and most of them have already arrived. The groom's brother-in-law—that is, our son Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu—has prepared the *urumaalai* to hang and decorate on the groom's head, the silk *veshti* to be worn with the sword-cloth, and the strong, handsome horse on which the groom is to be garlanded and brought in procession. The wedding news has already been announced to all the villages through Chozhan Thotti by the sound of the drum, by ‘*kuppaari kottuthal*.’ The groom's people have not only sent the customary basket of a *naazhi* of rice to our house; women relatives of the bride have also arrived to perform the ritual called ‘*pon poottik kulam othuthal*.’ The feast called ‘*pudukkalath unnal*’ has also taken place in our house for them. After all this, how can I look Chellaatha Gounder in the face and say that this wedding has stopped? Not only the groom's party who have come from Valanaadu—will not all the Kongu people gathered here from other regions shake with laughter when they hear our decision? Perumayi! If you say that, for the offence of having married you—and Thamarai! for the offence of having had you as my daughter—the honour of this Maniyangurichi Malaikkozhundhan must be sent sailing away, then I agree to it!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 38 → scan 39 -->

After speaking this way, his tongue would rise no further to speak. He took the silk *peethambaram* draped over his shoulder and wiped his eyes. Alarmed at his condition, Nelliyangodan clasped his feet tightly and said with emotion, “Maama! I do not want any marriage at all. I will remain a bachelor. Let no harm come to your family's honour because of me!” Thamarai Naachchi, unwilling to accept his act even by the smallest measure, forgot herself and cried out fiercely:

“Appa! Amma! Aththaan! The three of you come to whatever decision you want. But at the same time, hear my decision too. If I marry, I will marry only my Aththaan Nelliyangodan. Otherwise, according to your wish, when morning blooms, tell Maanthiyappan to tie the marriage cord around the corpse of this Thamarai Naachchi!”

Seeing the sparks blazing in Thamarai's eyes and sensing the resolve filling her heart, Malaikkozhundha Gounder stood utterly still.

“Thamarai!” Nelliyangodan interrupted, trying to stop her words.

“Keep quiet, Aththaan!” Thamarai glared at him once, and he stood without saying anything. Understanding the depth of Thamarai Naachchi's love for him, he was deeply moved.

Like the calm after a storm, silence settled in the room for a while.

Malaikkozhundha Gounder, standing like a pillar, moved slightly. He looked intently at Perumayi and Thamarai with his dimmed eyes.

“As you wish, the wedding of Nelliyangodan and Thamarai Naachchi will take place in the morning. I will go now and pacify Chellaatha Gounder.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 39 → scan 40 -->

Before he had even closed his mouth after saying this, mother and daughter, overwhelmed with joy, fell at his feet and bowed. Nelliyangodan alone stood scratching his head. Malaikkozhundha Gounder lifted those who had bowed at his feet, wiped away the tears of joy pouring from their eyes and said, “Live long! Live long!” before leaving the room. Thamarai embraced her mother and showered kisses upon her cheeks. Nelliyangodan, watching it all, gently stroked his own cheek as his whole body thrilled. Thamarai noticed this and her eyes reddened. Shyness seized her by the neck; lowering her head, she bounded like a deer onto her bed and buried her face in the pillow.

Realising there was no course other than to conduct the wedding of Nelliyangodan and Thamarai Naachchiyar, Malaikkozhundha Gounder entered the guest lodge where the groom's party was staying, his heart broken. Chellaatha Gounder was sleeping soundly; his servants went and woke him. Chellaatha Gounder sat up on the cot and ordered that Malaikkozhundha Gounder be brought inside. Entering with great respect, the man of Maniyangurichi understood Chellaatha Gounder's nod and sat on the seat there.

“What is the special matter at this hour?” Chellaatha Gounder's heavy, commanding voice sounded.

“Nothing. I came to ask your forgiveness.”

“Why? What mistake have you made? You have carried out very splendidly all the wedding customs that our Kongu people are to observe. Why then do you ask forgiveness?”

“I have left no deficiency in any of that!”

“Then what is the deficiency? Speak openly!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 40 → scan 41 -->

“I do not even know how to say it. All the dignitaries of the various regions have accepted my invitation and arrived for the wedding. Some more are still coming!”

“Yes! Yes! Oh, I forgot to tell you one thing! Our minor ruler of Menaadu, Thalaiyur Kaali, is also coming at my invitation! You have no objection to that, do you?”

“Mm... well... well...”

“Oh! It seems you do not like Thalaiyur's coming. You cannot bring yourself to say it, so you are chewing and swallowing your words like this! Man of Maniyangurichi, you must not forget one thing! The relationship that has grown in my time between Valanaadu and the young lion Thalaiyur Kaali is very strong. He is a very good young friend. At times, even if he does not like my view, because of his friendship for me he will not abandon me. He is an excellent friend who would do anything for me. I have invited him because it would not be good for my son's wedding to take place without him. A palm-leaf message has already come from him saying that he will arrive. Do not fail to arrange a suitable place for him to stay!”

“Do I not know about him? He is not merely a minor ruler, but a young man of unfailing courage that could toss mountains like balls; a friend who does not forget gratitude. No one can shake the friendship between you and him! I know all of it. His coming to the wedding is an honour to me as well. But the reason I came to see you at this hour is something else!”

Chellaatha Gounder stirred by the smallest measure, shook his body, stroked his moustache and, with eagerness gathered on his face, asked in a stern voice:

“What other reason?”

Malaikkozhundha Gounder's tongue went dry. Even so, he was determined to steady himself and explain the situation.

“Forgive me!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 41 → scan 42 -->

“If you say ‘forgive me’ without even telling me the matter, what am I to forgive? Why should I forgive?”

Chellaatha Gounder asked this question with both irritation and a shiver of agitation.

“Appa! I will tell him,” Maanthiyappan said, entering there with his friends like a man who had stepped on burning embers.

“It's nothing, Appa! This is the reason! Nelliyangodan has turned up from somewhere. He has decided to give his daughter to him in marriage.”

The instant these words came from Maanthiyappan's mouth with the heat of iron filings fallen deep into fire and brought out glowing, Chellaatha Gounder sprang up like a tiger with a cry of “Ah! What?”, seized the sword beside his bed and raised it toward Malaikkozhundha Gounder.

Like lightning, a hand shot out and stopped the grip of Chellaatha Gounder's sword. Everyone looked on in astonishment and alarm. Seeing that the one who had stopped him was Thalaiyur Kaali, they all stood motionless between joy and bewilderment.
