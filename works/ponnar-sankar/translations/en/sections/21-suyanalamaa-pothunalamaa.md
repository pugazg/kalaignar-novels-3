18

Self-Interest? Public Interest?

Before Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's bewilderment, surprise and hesitation could compete with one another and make him forget himself, auspicious music began to sound at the gate of Sankaranmalai Fort. Kundrudaiyaan was approaching, surrounded by *aalavattam*. Several warriors carrying swords and *vel* walked on either side of him. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu stood motionless, watching him come. Lines of brightness shone across the faces of Ponnar, Sankar, Veeramalai and the Aarichampatti warriors.

Realising that Kundrudaiyaan had come with his attendants to welcome them and lead them into the fort, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu and the others dismounted and stood looking at him. With a slight smile gathered on his lips, Kundrudaiyaan came and stood directly before Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu.

Though smiles played upon their lips, tears were peeping at the edges of both men's eyelids.

Both struggled, unable to speak. But at the same moment they embraced each other and for an instant looked like stone statues.

“Come, let us go inside! When those long separated are reunited, can they even speak? How many years have we been apart?” Kundrudaiyaan said in a soft, trembling voice, taking Chinna—

—malaik Kozhundhu's hands. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, overcome with emotion, walked with Kundrudaiyaan. Kundrudaiyaan's gaze did not fail to fall upon Ponnar and Sankar. The brave young men could not understand why, once that gaze had fallen upon them, it would not leave them and seemed to bind itself to them.

The two bowed their heads respectfully to Kundrudaiyaan in greeting, then followed Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu into the fort.

As they crossed the entrance, Kundrudaiyaan looked at two of his warriors and ordered, “Let food and lodging be prepared at once for everyone who has come from Aarichampatti!” Then he led Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu and the others toward the hall of the mansion inside the fort.

The fort and mansion were not vast structures built on a grand scale, yet they looked very strong. Since this was only a place where Kundrudaiyaan stayed from time to time for rest, Sankaranmalai Fort and mansion naturally stood second to the Kudaiyur mansion.

The moment they entered the mansion hall, an emotional event that sent a thrill through Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's entire body occurred suddenly, entirely beyond his expectation. Yes—his younger sister Thamarai Naachchiyar and her daughter Arukkaani Thangam ran to him, fell at his feet and wept in torrents. Crying out, “Thamarai!” Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu poured out a brother's affection and lifted her to her feet.

Though many years had passed, the anger buried deep in their hearts and the force of the scorching words they had exchanged because of it all fell away in an instant. When they looked at each other face to face, only affection rose high between them.

“Thamarai! This is Vaiyam Perumaan, my son. These are my daughters, Muthayi and Pavalayi!” Before he could finish, Thamarai Naachchiyar, with enthusiasm overflowing like a flood, exclaimed, “Of course I know! We saw them at the Chellandiyamman temple festival!” and pulled the two girls close, embracing them.

“My name is Arutkanni! My mother and father—why, everyone—fondly call me Arukkaani Thangam,” Arukkaani Thangam hurried to say. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu went to her affectionately, stroked her head and kissed the crown of it. By then Silambayi had drawn Arukkaani toward herself and embraced her.

Like Kundrudaiyaan's eyes, Thamarai Naachchi's eyes too did not fail to gather around Ponnar and Sankar. Seeing two families that had long been enemies come together in happiness, Maayavar stood aside in a corner, silently enjoying the scene. Indeed, more than the eyes of Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchiyar, it was Maayavar's eyes that never moved even a little from Ponnar and Sankar, drinking in their appearance, beauty and majestic bearing.

They too looked at Maayavar in surprise. Was he not the man who had come upon them that day while they were fighting wild boars at Maarik Goundan Paalayam and asked the way to their teacher's camp? They wondered what had brought him here.

“What is this, Maayavar? Why are you standing aside?” Kundrudaiyaan asked as he approached him. Then, looking at Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, he asked, “Brother-in-law! Do you know him?” Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's embarrassed face showed his struggle to understand immediately.

“He is Thalaiyur Kaali's minister. His name is Maayavar. Have you never seen him? Have you not even heard of him?”

At Kundrudaiyaan's question, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu collected himself.

“Ah! Him? Have I merely heard of him? I have seen him! His beard and moustache have changed him so much now that he is almost unrecognisable. For many years he had left our southern country itself...” Before he could finish, Maayavar smiled at him and replied:

“After wandering through many regions, I have returned to Thalaiyur itself!”

“Everyone, sit down! You must be tired. Let us first have some refreshments while we talk,” Kundrudaiyaan said, beginning to play host.

Everyone sat on the seats in that beautiful hall of the mansion.

Seeing Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai still standing, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu said with overflowing affection, “Why? You too, sit down!” Once again, every pair of eyes circled around Ponnar and Sankar.

The way Thamarai Naachchi's eyes looked at them was like hands digging through beach sand to recover pearl-studded earrings that had been lost on the shore and buried beneath the earth.

The way Kundrudaiyaan's eyes looked at them was as though he were asking in wonder how his own form had split into two and appeared in the full-length mirror before him.

Maayavar's eyes looked at them. Those eyes were giving off a clear light.

“Was it not because you wanted to have *archana* performed in these young men's names that you went about asking for their names at the Chellandiyamman temple festival?” Kundrudaiyaan asked Vaiyam Perumaan, Muthayi and Pavalayi. Nelliyangodan was a man of such open-hearted innocence that, without the least blemish in his mind, he recalled the incident before everyone.

Too shy to risk giving any detailed answer, Muthayi and Pavalayi merely nodded their heads as if to say, “Yes.” Vaiyam Perumaan nodded too.

‘They went about trying to have *archana* performed in our names? Oh! Then love for us had already begun to bloom in these beauties of Aarichampatti even then!’ Ponnar and Sankar luxuriated in that delightful thought. Like horses that break restraint and gallop on, refusing even when ropes pull them back, their gazes sprang toward Muthayi and Pavalayi.

In a place crowded with many people, are not the eyes the ones that have learned the art of becoming the lips and tongues of loving hearts, speaking without anyone else knowing? Those eyes performed that work now as well.

“I too have heard that these young fellows come and go from this mansion. Their teacher Raakkiyannan of Maarik Goundan Paalayam comes and goes from this fort whenever he wishes. It seems they sometimes come with him too.”

As Kundrudaiyaan began telling them about the young men, Maayavar interrupted. “But they will not tell anyone their names!” Then, looking at them, he asked with a smile, “Is that not so, thambis?”

“Yes,” Ponnar and Sankar answered, merely nodding their heads.

“Why will you not tell your names, boys? Could it be that your parents never gave you names at all?”

Maayavar would not let them off and pressed them with this question.

“Our teacher Raakkiyannan ordered us not to tell our names to anyone!” Ponnar said.

At that moment Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu cleared his throat lightly and made as though to say something.

Just then Arukkaani Thangam came with four or five women carrying refreshments and personally took the lead in having them served to those present. During that time Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu whispered something secretly into the ear of his wife Silambayi and kept looking at her face for her response. After thinking for a moment, Silambayi whispered a question of doubt to her husband. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu gave the answer into her ear as well. It was clear that Silambayi thought deeply. Then she nodded to him and whispered her consent into his ear.

After the women who had served the refreshments left the place, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu cleared his throat again and began to speak.

“Though these young men will not tell us their names, I can already see that they are going to establish names for themselves in this world! They are living examples of the saying that one can know the crop from the sprout. At the festival of releasing the *karagam* at the Chellandiyamman temple, it was they who saved my daughters, who otherwise might have been swept away with the cloth into the Kaveri by the wind. And that is not all. Maanthiyappan, son of Chellaatha Gounder, with the help of Thalaiyur commander Thirumalai, waylaid my daughters and took them to Raachchaandaar Malai. If not for these two lion cubs, my daughters' lives too would have been destroyed.

<!-- source boundary: scan 178 → scan 179 -->

“The Aarichampatti family too would have been wiped out. Because they defeated Thirumalai at Raachchaandaar Malai and rescued my daughters, I have announced that I will give my daughters to them in marriage. Now I am bound to fulfil my promise. In the presence of the good and capable Maayavar, and before my beloved brother-in-law and dear younger sister, I ask everyone to give their consent to my decision!”

When Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu finished speaking, Maayavar asked:

“You yourself announced a prize, and you yourself have made the decision too. Have you learned what your daughters think? Or have you at least asked what these young men think?”

Before Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu could answer Maayavar's question, the cheeks of the two pairs—Ponnar and Muthayi, Sankar and Pavalayi—reddened, a new light arose in their eyes, and in the language of silence itself they had already expressed their consent.

“Raakkiyannar is our teacher! Nothing can happen without asking for his consent,” Veeramalai said, throwing a stone into the matter.

Upon that stone Thamarai Naachchiyar threw another, still larger stone.

“Anna! Have you forgotten the oath I made on the day of my wedding? I swore: ‘You will see me and my husband living in a storied mansion! I shall give birth to two lion-cub sons. You too will have two daughters, and you will have to seek out those lion cubs and bring them!’ Anna, even if you have forgotten it, I will not forget!”

There was fierceness in Thamarai Naachchiyar's speech. At the same time, as the old enmity disappeared, affection was surging rapidly.

Looking at Thamarai, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu asked in a gentle voice:

“Why, Thamarai? I too wish for your oath to be fulfilled. If it were fulfilled, the long-standing hostility between our families would be over. But it is true that those lion cubs you spoke of that day were born. And it is also true that they died, is it not, Thamarai?”

On hearing this, Thamarai Naachchiyar became agitated.

“Who said so? My children are alive. I will see them soon. Anna! You must change your decision and give your daughters only to my sons. Even if you refuse, I will fulfil my vow somehow!”

Thamarai flared.

Then Maayavar, raising his voice slightly, created a new confusion: “So now the question has become this, has it not? Is Thamarai's vow to be fulfilled, or is Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's promise to be fulfilled?”
