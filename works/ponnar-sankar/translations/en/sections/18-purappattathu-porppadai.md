15

The War Host Sets Out

Following Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's roar, the great drum at the entrance of Aarichampatti Fort thundered. Since the sound of that drum was known to be an emergency summons from the palace, the warriors resting in the camps, along with the brave young men of the region who joined the army in times of war, girded themselves, took up their weapons and gathered at the fort gate.

On a high platform built of black stone before the entrance, where all those standing in formation could see him, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu stood like fury embodied. In the light of the torches burning in rows, the tips of the warriors' spears, the edges of their swords and the fronts of their shields glittered brightly. Holding a broad, long, heavy sword raised in his right hand, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu began to address the troops surrounding him.

He was not merely a volcano overflowing with anger. Because of the great danger that had befallen his wife and children, and because of the challenge thrown at Aarichampatti, the words that came from his shattered heart fell like embers, yet trembled as they came.

“Chellaatha Gounder of Nelli Valanaadu has decided to clash with Aarichampatti. With Thalaiyur Kaali at his side, he has challenged us in the contemptuous belief that Aarichampatti is only a very small territory.

<!-- source boundary: scan 147 → scan 148 -->

“Nelli Valanaadu is a larger country than ours. They rule a broader stretch of land than we do, and they possess many times our military strength. That is why, all these years, we have tried to remain on good terms with them and prevent a break in relations. But they have kept in mind the thwarting of Thamarai Naachchiyar's marriage and have tried to create many opportunities to spring upon us. Finally, they argued that Raachchaandaar Malai belonged to Valanaadu, citing various old documents as evidence. Even though those documents did not seem wholly trustworthy to us, we did not make a great issue of it. Wanting to avoid a needless war, we yielded Raachchaandaar Malai to them and averted battle. For two years Chellaatha Gounder has sent many envoys asking for both Muthayi and Pavalayi in marriage for his son Maanthiyappan. The people of Valanaadu kept insisting that because my younger sister Thamarai Naachchi was not married to him, he had remained unmarried altogether, and that as compensation for the humiliation they suffered at Aarichampatti, both my daughters should be given to Maanthiyappan. I rejected his demand outright, saying there could be no question of marrying two women to one man. When I wondered whether I might at least give one of the two to Maanthiyappan, the reports that came about him were terrifying—terrifying indeed! Even now the reports about him are the same. They say he is always with a cup of liquor in his hand. In that intoxication, whenever he sees any woman, he thinks she must come at once to his bed. They say women who fall under his gaze tremble in fear. How could I agree to give my daughter to such a human beast? That is why I refused—not merely to give two women, but even one. Yet I never realised that Chellaatha Gounder had kept my refusal in his heart and was waiting for the right moment to take revenge.

<!-- source boundary: scan 148 → scan 149 -->

“Had I known, I would have sent a great force as protection when my family returned from the Chellandiyamman temple festival. These young lions standing here—I do not know their names, but I can never forget them. They are the ones who saved my daughters from becoming victims of the Kaveri during the festival of releasing the *karagam*. I brought them with me and returned to the palace earlier. After the festival, my wife, my son Vaiyam Perumaan, and my daughters Muthayi and Pavalayi worshipped the Goddess and were returning to the palace when Thalaiyur Kaali's men blocked their way near Kulithalai and took them by another route to Raachchaandaar Thirumalai! Our warriors fought them and became corpses. One surviving warrior came here grievously wounded, delivered the news, and he too died a warrior's death. According to the plan announced by Thalaiyur Kaali's commander Thirumalai, my family will be imprisoned at Raachchaandaar Malai. Attempts will be made to conduct forced marriages between Maanthiyappan and my daughters. Neither my daughters, nor my wife, nor my son will consent! Rather than accept that marriage, they would choose to take their own lives and die. Now you must tell me what I should do. That is why I called you. Do you ask me to send a palm-leaf message to Chellaatha Gounder saying, ‘Let us make peace; as you demanded, I shall make my daughters Maanthiyappan's wives’? Or do you ask me to confront them, whatever may happen? Shall we submit? Or shall we march to war?”

When Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu had finished explaining and asked that final question, the spears and swords surrounding him rose and waved toward the sky.

“We march to war! We will not submit!” came a single united cry, swelling like a great chant.

<!-- source boundary: scan 149 → scan 150 -->

“They are strong in numbers. Do not forget that Thalaiyur Kaali too stands beside them as an ally!” Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu said, as though warning them.

“We do not care about defeat! If we are defeated, let it be written in the blood pouring from our chests. Even if the word ‘surrender’ were engraved upon the tablets of our backs with carving stones, there would be no pride in it. It would only be a mark of shame!”

A voice rang out. There was the note of valour in it. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu stared toward the place from which it came, and everyone turned to look.

It was Veeramalai Sambuvan who had spoken. Ponnar and Sankar looked at him with smiles. Both embraced him. The warriors gathered at the entrance of the fort gazed without taking their eyes off the three young men.

Ponnar and Sankar thought of the disaster that had now befallen Muthayi and Pavalayi, whom they had saved from the whirlwind, the mountain snake and the crocodile. They realised that rescuing them now would mean facing a great war, and they waited to see whether they too would be called into the battle to save them.

It was because Veeramalai knew that they were in just such a state of mind that he had looked at Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu and declared with emphatic firmness that the word “surrender” was a mark of shame.

Though he ruled only a very small territory, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu must be said to have acted as a remarkable statesman.

Otherwise, after learning that his family had been taken prisoner, preparing for war and sounding the drum to summon his people, would he have gathered those who answered the call and then asked them themselves, “Shall we go to war—or not?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 150 → scan 151 -->

As the success of that statesmanship, the warriors and citizens of Aarichampatti declared that there could be no question of “submission” and stood waiting for Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's command. Even so, he knew very well that what awaited him, his family and the people under his rule in that war was heroic death and defeat.

His one hope was this: if they went at once and attacked Raachchaandaar Malai, the force facing the Aarichampatti army from there would consist only of Thalaiyur commander Thirumalai, the troops already with him, and the Nelli Valanaadu force alongside them.

Defeating Thalaiyur commander Thirumalai would not be easy. Even so, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu calculated that if the Aarichampatti army could enter Raachchaandaar Malai before the whole Thalaiyur army arrived and joined battle, there would be some chance of victory.

An inner impulse urged him, in any case, to gain the help of those three young men and thereby add further strength to his army.

Using Veeramalai's valour-brimming words themselves as a rope ladder by which to draw close to the young men, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu looked at Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai and called, “All three of you, come up onto this platform. Let our people see your radiant faces, your broad and powerful shoulders, and your eyes that would not blink even if spears and lances came flying at you on the battlefield! Please, come!”

Though their shoulders were yearning to enter the battle to rescue from captivity the beloved vines who had taken residence in their hearts, unfurled their flags there and held sway, it was true that Ponnar felt shy about saying it aloud because of his younger brother Sankar, just as Sankar hesitated and faltered because of his elder brother Ponnar.

<!-- source boundary: scan 151 → scan 152 -->

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's invitation came as though to remove that feeling of shyness and hesitation.

The two looked at each other, and then both looked together at Veeramalai. Veeramalai, who had clearly read what was written in their hearts, let a smile show and signalled with his eyes that they should go with him to the platform.

When the three climbed onto the platform and stood there, it looked as though three tiger cubs surrounded Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu. Those gathered around raised their weapons and cheered in delight.

Pointing to Veeramalai, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu asked, “What did you say? You said that defeat should be written in the blood pouring from our chests, and that even if the word ‘surrender’ were engraved upon the tablets of our backs with carving stones, there would be no pride in it. Did you not? Thambi, there is only one way for me to enter the field having determined that there shall be no place at all for the word ‘defeat’ or the word ‘surrender’, and that victory alone is certain—a trustworthy way, a way with not even a sesame-tip's worth of doubt. You three can open that way for me! Will you open that way?”

Although Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu asked the question while looking at Veeramalai, Ponnar and Sankar both raised their hands at the same moment to signal their agreement.

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's enthusiasm overflowed its banks. The Aarichampatti army would attack the battlements of Raachchaandaar Malai Fort. Aarichampatti might meet defeat in the counterattack. Heroic death might be the result. He had already resolved that, whatever happened, he would face it with a firm heart. Now that he also had the support of Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai, not the slightest doubt arose in him about the outcome of the campaign.

<!-- source boundary: scan 152 → scan 153 -->

“Warriors, make ready at once!” Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu ordered. Before stepping down from the platform at the entrance of the fort, he issued another proclamation. Before making it, he drew Ponnar against one shoulder and Sankar against the other.

“If we win this campaign, break Chellaatha Gounder's plot, and rescue my family imprisoned at Raachchaandaar Malai, I pledge to make my daughter Muthayi the life-companion of this young man here, and Pavalayi the life-companion of this young man here! These two will take responsibility as commanders of our force setting out toward Raachchaandaar Malai!”

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's announcement was met with a single roar of joy. Aarichampatti Fort itself shook with the great cry, “Veera Vel! Vetri Vel!”

Ponnar and Sankar went with Veeramalai into the Aarichampatti armoury to take the weapons and put on the dress belonging to commanders.

They came out almost as quickly as they had entered, dressed for war and carrying drawn swords. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu waited for them on horseback. Horses stood ready for Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai, and the three leapt onto them and took their seats.

Looking grandly over the warriors drawn up in formation, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu raised his sword and cried aloud, “Veera Vel!” A great roar of “Vetri Vel!” rose like thunder. The army set out from Aarichampatti Fort.

Torch-bearers, swordsmen, archers, men bearing *vel*, men bearing spears—all on horseback—filled the road toward Raachchaandaar Malai, raising dust along the path in the darkness.

<!-- source boundary: scan 153 → scan 154 -->

Because Raachchaandaar Malai was not very far from Aarichampatti by the direct route, the cavalry had entered that region before dawn.

The news astonished Thirumalai, commander of Thalaiyur, who had believed that the people of Aarichampatti would come only to make peace and would not bring an army.

Without delaying even for a moment, he issued orders to his soldiers.

Thalaiyur Kaali's soldiers, famed as warriors whom no one could defeat in archery, began to rain arrows upon the Aarichampatti army from within Raachchaandaar Malai Fort.

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu was stunned by the assault of arrows pouring down like the great drops of a summer rain. Ponnar and Sankar, the commanders advancing toward the fort with the warriors, hesitated for a moment and pulled back on their horses' reins.
