4

A Pleasant Setting and a New Sorrow

“Who? Thalaiyur Thambi?”

Even as Chellaatha Gounder raised that question in surprise, he stood with his head bowed, thinking of the humiliation he had suffered in Maniyangurichi.

A teak-like body; a moustache like a scorpion's sting; eyes that flashed like a sharpened sword; a tall bearing; a crown-like turban of silk set with fine navaratna gems; youthful vigour that made him seem only twenty or twenty-five; and, amid all this, a facial expression showing that he possessed a mind capable of examining anything calmly, thinking it through and reaching a decision—Thalaiyur Kaali, the minor ruler of Menaadu who possessed all these qualities, gently placed his hand on Chellaatha Gounder's shoulder.

“What has happened that you would dare go so far as to turn the very man who is to give his daughter in marriage into a corpse?” he asked.

Chellaatha Gounder, who by the strength of his scheming mind had driven Nelliyangodan, a man with a heart as soft as cotton, out of Valanaadu, now sought to draw Thalaiyur Kaali fully to his own side in this situation and use him to frighten Malaikkozhundha Gounder into submission. He therefore began pouring out a thoroughly filtered lie.

“I told him that I had sent an invitation to the minor ruler of Menaadu, Thalaiyur Kaali, and that he too was coming to the wedding.

<!-- source boundary: scan 43 → scan 44 -->

At once the man of Maniyangurichi began leaping from mountain to pit. He angrily demanded to know whom I had asked before sending the invitation. I said he was my dear friend and that I needed to ask no one before inviting him to my son's wedding. He then spoke disgracefully, saying that if Thalaiyur Kaali was going to attend the wedding, he could not give his daughter Thamarai in marriage to Maanthiyappan and that everyone might as well return home at once! Thalaiyur Thambi, I would not mind if he had spoken insultingly about me—but he belittled before my very eyes the minor ruler of Menaadu, who stands as Valanaadu's close friend. After that, should his head remain on his shoulders? That is why I swung the sword! His life was strong; because you intervened, he escaped!”

The moment Chellaatha Gounder's answer reached his ears, those present expected Thalaiyur Kaali, because of his youthful vigour, to leap up in fury. Instead, without moving, he smiled.

“To the major landholder of Maniyangurichi, my coming to this celebration may have seemed an ordinary matter. He may have thought that my coming would diminish his prestige. Or he may have thought that, by inviting a minor ruler like me, he would be unable to find a place for me to stay in this village or provide suitable arrangements. Should you magnify such a small matter into something enormous and come forward to make an inauspicious place of the very place where an auspicious celebration is being held?”

In Thalaiyur Kaali's measured speech, royal dignity stood foremost.

Realising with alarm that Chellaatha Gounder had woven a web of deceit, Malaikkozhundha Gounder approached Thalaiyur Kaali and, in a faltering voice, asked, “Do you believe this?”

“I am one who considers the very purpose of my birth to be believing whatever Chellaatha Gounder says!

<!-- source boundary: scan 44 → scan 45 -->

If he has become so angry with you that he raised a heavy sword against you, I know that the reason is the boundless love and respect he has for me. Man of Maniyangurichi! But one thing: I have not the slightest hurt or anger that you did not invite me, nor that you asked Chellaatha Gounder why he invited me. The only distress in my mind is that, with me at the centre of it, a quarrel has arisen between you, and because of that the Maanthiyappan-Thamarai wedding itself is being stopped.”

Taking the hands of Thalaiyur Kaali, who had explained himself calmly, the man of Maniyangurichi said, “Please listen to what I have to say. You know that Nelliyangodan is my nephew by marriage. Thamarai is his customary bride. He suddenly returned from somewhere. My daughter is stubbornly saying that if she marries, she will marry only him. I came to Chellaatha Gounder only to explain this and pacify him. He is telling you an entirely different story,” and laid out the truth.

On a stage overgrown with the *nerunjil* thorns of falsehood, can the dance performance of truth be staged?

Chellaatha Gounder, determined that such a thing should not happen even by mistake, used without the slightest deviation the skill he possessed both in investigation and in the craft of incitement.

“Man of Maniyangurichi! I ask you even now. I ask you for the last time. If it is true that you bear no resentment over the arrival of my friend and Valanaadu's powerful ally, the distinguished Thalaiyur Kaali, but instead are immersed in happiness—are you prepared to conduct the marriage that was fixed, exactly as it was fixed?”

Unable to answer this question—no, not a question, but a challenge—Malaikkozhundha Gounder struggled for words.

<!-- source boundary: scan 45 → scan 46 -->

The major landholder of Maniyangurichi, who looked like a mountain that had taken human form, stroked the thick moustache spread across his face and seemed to shrink in on himself as he said, “That decision is no longer in my hands.”

“You who do not have the courage or strength to make a decision—why did you first agree to give your daughter into such a great family without learning the bride's wishes? Imagine the present situation turned completely upside down! Suppose your daughter had thought of Maanthiyappan in her heart as her bridegroom and was waiting in the morning for the wedding, and then Maanthiyappan changed his mind and said he was going to marry another woman—how would your heart have boiled? So reconsider the decision you made in anger and haste because of my arrival. If you think your honour will be diminished because of me, I can even keep my mouth shut without offering any blessing at all. Do not ruin a good event on my account.”

While Thalaiyur Kaali was saying this to the man of Maniyangurichi, Chellaatha Gounder seized Thalaiyur Kaali by the shoulder, shook him and said, “Ayyo! What injustice is this? Must you humble yourself so far and plead on my account? No, never! There can be no more talk of a marriage alliance with this house! Thalaiyur Thambi, it is certainly true that some terrible scheme lies hidden behind the end of this arrogance! We shall learn what it is as time goes on. I will tell you about it then. Otherwise, this Maniyangurichi Malaikkozhundha Gounder would never have found the courage to oppose even you.” The moment he said this, Maanthiyappan, like a volcano that had been rumbling until then and suddenly burst, sprang up in rage.

“After matters have gone this far, even standing in this house any longer is an affront to our honour!

<!-- source boundary: scan 46 → scan 47 -->

If even a mustard-seed's worth of opposition is shown to the minor kingdom of Thalaiyur, my heart will not allow me to accept that enemy as a relative by marriage! Even if the daughter of the man of Maniyangurichi were some great Rambha or Urvashi, even if she herself came now and fell at my feet, I would not take her as my wife. But one thing: Maniyangurichi will very soon have to answer for the humiliation that happened today. It will very soon have to experience the consequence of this in a terrible way.”

Breathing hard with agitation, he roared, “Come, Appa; let us return to Valanaadu!”

“Can what is called fate be rewritten once it exists?” Thalaiyur Kaali murmured, looking up at the roof of the guest pavilion. Before taking leave of Chellaatha Gounder and departing, he turned toward the man of Maniyangurichi and, in a slightly mocking tone that revealed a little of the bitterness in his mind, said, “Thank you for tolerating my remaining among you for so long.”

Embracing Maanthiyappan, Kaali said, “The decision you have taken is unavoidable. I welcome it. There is no other way. Make arrangements to return at once with the relatives. This is a house in which an auspicious event is to take place in the morning, so you must behave peacefully without any untoward incident. This is the request of Menaadu, which is friendly with Valanaadu. Is that understood?” With that he left.

Everyone staying in the guest mansion with Chellaatha Gounder prepared for the journey. Amid that crowd and the bustle of getting ready to travel, they spoke among themselves about the man of Maniyangurichi in tones of mockery, teasing, opposition and derision. None of it reached clearly the ears of Malaikkozhundha Gounder, who stood there motionless. Before dawn,

<!-- source boundary: scan 47 → scan 48 -->

all the people of Valanaadu had left Maniyangurichi.

Before sunrise the news had spread throughout the region. At daybreak, after learning all these details, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu went to his father and warned him that the consequences of acting according to Thamarai's wish would be disastrous. He poured fire upon his sister Thamarai: “Of all people, you have chosen to marry a madman; not only are you going to ruin yourself, you have darkened the future of Maniyangurichi too.” Still seething, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu paced back and forth, while his mother and sister, without replying to him, busied themselves with the wedding arrangements. There was no trace of happiness on the father's face. Though the mother's face was full with thoughts of her daughter's wedding, one could still see on it that some feeling moved in her heart like a line running through a cat's-eye gem.

Whatever else might happen, Thamarai Naachchiyar, thrilled that she was going to marry her Aththaan Nelliyangodan just as she had wished, appeared with a face like a lotus blooming wide in cool water.

Nelliyangodan's face was drawn and sorrowful like a jasmine bud, yet his heart was filled with joy like the fragrance hidden within that bud.

When Chozhan Thotti, following the custom of the Kongu people, announced the sudden change in the wedding by *kuppaari kottuthal* and spread the news as far as he could before the whole night had passed, people gathered in groups here and there and became intensely busy exchanging views about the marriage.

Chozhan Thotti had a special enthusiasm for making the announcement by *kuppaari kottuthal*. The reason was that it was on Chozhan Thotti's idea that Nelliyangodan had been able to enter Maniyangurichi in disguise. Arrangements were now being made for him to marry the woman his heart desired.

<!-- source boundary: scan 48 → scan 49 -->

So when Chozhan Thotti, with fresh vigour and all weariness gone, spun about like a top, Nelliyangodan heaved a joyful sigh, thinking what a deeply loyal heart he possessed.

For a wedding among the Kongu people to take place according to the auspicious-blessing custom said to have been sung by Kambar, many people had to be present—the groom's household, the bride's household and their relatives. But in this wedding Chozhan Thotti himself now had to act on behalf of the groom's household and gather relatives to take part in the rites belonging to the groom. Most of those who had come from Valanaadu for the wedding had left with Chellaatha Gounder when he departed. Yet some close relatives of Nelliyangodan—those related as elder and younger brothers, elder and younger sisters, and through various other kinship relationships—had remained in Maniyangurichi out of sympathy, saying, “Poor boy, born to a family that once lived in authority! If only his father Kolaatha Gounder or his mother Pavalaathaal had been alive, how splendidly this wedding would have taken place!” Even the relatives who agreed to participate did not fail to praise the effort Chozhan Thotti made in that urgent hour to approach them, obtain their consent and bring them into the auspicious wedding rites.

Somehow, at the appointed time, the wedding ceremonies began to take on their full festal form. Once relatives from both households had gathered in the pavilion, the *muhurtha kaal*, called the first *seer*, was planted. Nine kinds of grain were tied in a cloth soaked in turmeric and fastened to the *muhurtha kaal*, made from a branch of the peepal tree; the *arumaikkarar* poured milk over it and completed the prescribed worship. The groom Nelliyangodan was seated in the wedding chamber and the bachelor-vow rite was completed. Under the custom called tying the betel bundle, betel leaves, areca nut, coconut, banana, mirror, comb, finger turmeric, lemon, flowers, sandal paste, the wedding sari and blouse cloth

<!-- source boundary: scan 49 → scan 50 -->

and other items were tied into a bundle in a *veshti*. The *arumaikkarar* performed worship over it; it was then carried with music to the Pillaiyar temple, brought back, placed in the wedding chamber, circled with water and opened, and the bride Thamarai Naachchi was made to receive it. Following custom, she put on the betel-bundle wedding sari and rose after bowing to three elders. Nelliyangodan was seated on a three-legged stool, red rice was circled around him and water poured as a rite to ward off the evil eye. A knotless finger-turmeric was tied to a thread smeared with turmeric; incense and lamps were shown to it, and the *arumaikkarar*, also called the *arumaip periyor*, tied it to the right hands of both bride and groom. Surrounded by relatives, the groom was seated on a horse and, with drums and music sounding, went to the Pillaiyar temple, broke a coconut, worshipped and returned. The bride's household came to the place where the Kongu elders known as the *naattaar sabai* had gathered and completed the customary invitation ceremony by saying, “Come, all of you.” All those standing to the bride in the maternal-uncle relationship were invited, given new *veshti*, anointed with sandal paste and offered milk and fruit. A full *naazhi* was circled, five cakes of red rice were removed, and an *arumaikkarar* woman completed the rite of warding off the evil eye for the bride.

The wedding chamber was decorated with flowers. Banana and areca palms had been tied there. In the centre stood two *karagam* vessels, one filled with paddy and the other with water. Before them were a Pillaiyar fashioned from turmeric, a thousand-wick lamp, *boothakala* rice, betel and areca nut, and a heap of moulded jaggery.

When the groom Nelliyangodan, who had been brought on horseback, reached the wedding chamber, the ceremony of tying the *mangala naan* began. A man known as the *kudimagan* sang the song called “Kambar Mangala Vizha Vaazhthu” in a firm voice without spoiling the purity of the words, and the whole pavilion was filled with emotion. With concern that every rite should be carried out according to custom,

<!-- source boundary: scan 50 → scan 51 -->

Perumayi Ammal and, supporting her, Malaikkozhundha Gounder took part, though in the rush one or two customary rites had to be performed slightly out of their usual order. Everyone who had come to witness the wedding understood that the main reason was the sudden turn the marriage had taken.

The *arumaikkarar* placed the bride Thamarai Naachchi facing east and the groom Nelliyangodan facing west toward her, and had them exchange garlands. After the *thanganam* was removed, Nelliyangodan received with great reverence the *mangala naan* given by the *arumaip periyor* and tied it around Thamarai Naachchi's neck as auspicious sounds filled the pavilion.

The auspicious blessing song begun by the man called the *kudimagan* continued to resound.

Overflowing with happiness, the newly married couple fell at the feet of Malaikkozhundha Gounder and Perumayi Ammal, bowed and received their blessings. Tears of joy streamed down Perumayi Ammal's cheeks. Malaikkozhundha Gounder's face looked like a mirror whose silvering had faded, impossible to read for the waves of thought striking within him. After bowing at her parents' feet, Thamarai went toward her brother Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu to fall at his feet. Until then he had taken part in the event only half-heartedly. Unable even to remember that they were in a pavilion crowded with hundreds of relatives and friends, anger choking his throat, he shouted, “Chi! You uncontrollable shrew!” and stormed out with the speed of a cyclone. A terrible sorrow settled over the wedding pavilion that had moments before held such a pleasant atmosphere. Worried about where Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's fury would end, Perumayi Ammal cried aloud, “Periya Kaandiyamma, you alone are our refuge!”
