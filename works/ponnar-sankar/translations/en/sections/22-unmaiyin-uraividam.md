19

The Abode of Truth

“If my younger sister's vow is to be fulfilled, the children she bore must be alive now!”

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu asked this not only looking at Maayavar, but also casting a sidelong glance at Thamarai.

Preparing to answer the question fully, Maayavar cleared his throat and straightened himself.

“I believe that the children born to Thamarai are alive. If they are, then all of us together must help Thamarai Naachchi's vow be fulfilled.”

Hearing this preface from Maayavar, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu was slightly shaken.

“How can that be? Merely on the assumption that those children may be alive, and because we believe it so, would it be just for me to abandon the promise I gave midway? What will these young warriors think of the worth of my word?”

Understanding how deeply Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu cared that the purity of his pledged word should suffer no blemish, Maayavar said:

“You are trying to go around in circles to find out what those young men think! Why should either you or I struggle? Let the young men themselves say: should the promise be kept, or should the vow—

<!-- source boundary: scan 181 → scan 182 -->

—be fulfilled?” Thus he posed them a riddle.

Ponnar then looked at Sankar's face. With his gaze, Sankar made it clear that Ponnar had the right to speak on his behalf. So Ponnar himself came forward to find a solution to the problem.

“Neither I nor my younger brother received that promise as a boon. The ruler of Aarichampatti made such a proclamation of his own accord. Nor did we ask for such a promise and then receive it. No one need suffer as Dasaratha did after giving promises to Kaikeyi. Nothing we did for them was done expecting repayment. So, on behalf of myself and my younger brother, I say openly: neither of us will insist upon the promise.”

When these words came from Ponnar, Muthayi and Pavalayi were deeply disappointed.

Let the promise lie aside—what would become of the love they had nurtured? Ah! How casually these young warriors had brushed the two of them aside! Even if Thamarai Naachchiyar's sons were to appear before them saying, “Here we are, alive!”, what could that change? Could they alter their hearts for that? Had their aunt's sons perhaps appeared before they met these two, that would have been another matter. But faster and more completely than the nourishment from food mingles with the blood, the greatness of their love for these marvellous young men had become inseparably mingled with every atom of their bodies and the full expanse of their hearts. And now, in such a state, these dear men were stepping back, saying they would not insist upon their father's promise. How could that be just?

<!-- source boundary: scan 182 → scan 183 -->

Thus Muthayi and Pavalayi both thought, their hearts swelling with emotion.

From a distance, Maayavar understood the storm in the girls' minds from the changes in their faces. A faint smile traced itself across his face. In that smile flickered a playful mischief tempered by maturity. He placed a hand on Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's shoulder. Out of the respect he held for Maayavar as the minister of the King of Thalaiyur, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu softened. Maayavar let his eyes sweep once around everyone and began to speak.

“We cannot simply value Thamarai Naachchiyar's feelings lightly and set them aside. It is good that the ruler of Aarichampatti too should know the truths I learned by asking Thamarai Naachchiyar and her beloved husband Kundrudaiyaan. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu may have been angry because Thamarai's decision that day caused the proposed marriage alliance with Chellaatha Gounder's house to collapse suddenly and earned the hostility of Thalaiyur Kaali. But the sufferings Thamarai and Kundrudaiyaan have endured since leaving the wedding hall that day are no small thing. This daughter of the Kongu Vellalar clan held fast to her principle, crossed so many hardships and sorrows that confronted her, and has now risen to the point where she welcomes with horses in attendance the very elder brother's family that once drove her and her husband from the house. Is this not a victory won by the strength of womanhood?”

Maayavar's own words sent a thrill through his body. Even his voice trembled slightly. At that moment Thamarai Naachchiyar raised her face toward the sky so no one would see the tears budding in her eyes. Maayavar did not stop speaking.

“A queenly woman born in a palace! Rather than change the love she held for Nelliyangodan and become another man's wife—

<!-- source boundary: scan 183 → scan 184 -->

—she made herself a woman of sacrifice. She cast aside as worthless the comfortable life in which servants laboured over lands stretching beyond the eye and brought home heaps of grain. Instead, beside her husband, she herself toiled under the burning sun in field, scrub and upland, becoming an example of the dignity of farming people. How can one describe the pain of such a noblewoman suffering after losing the children she bore? People speak of a child obtained through penance. In her case it was no conventional phrase: this Naachchiyar truly undertook austerities and subjected herself to hardship. In this world filled with days of deception, Kundrudaiyaan trusted things easily. By her strength and intelligence, Thamarai Naachchiyar protected him and raised him to be a leader over the hill-filled regions, admired by many. Yet she was deeply troubled that no lamps of succession had appeared for their family. For that, there was no sacred water they did not seek and no temple they did not worship at. Knowing the grief they suffered without children, do you know what Chellaatha Gounder did?”

Maayavar asked the question himself, and before he answered it himself, everyone looked eagerly at his face. Thamarai Naachchiyar lowered her head. Kundrudaiyaan too listened with interest as Maayavar turned back and read aloud the past pages of his life's record. Maayavar continued.

“Chellaatha Gounder prepared a few astrologers and sent them to these two. Not knowing where the astrologers came from, the couple welcomed and hosted them, paid the fees and other things they asked for, and asked whether they had the fortune of children. The astrologers lied that if they made a chariot for Madhukkarai Chelliyamman, adorned the Goddess in it and held a chariot procession, they would immediately be blessed with children.

<!-- source boundary: scan 184 → scan 185 -->

“Believing that, Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchiyar immediately began making arrangements to build a chariot for Chelliyamman. Seeing this as the moment to take revenge on Thamarai and Kundrudaiyaan, who had fallen for his scheme, Chellaatha Gounder caught hold of one of the carpenters working on the chariot and planted another plot. A terrible conspiracy was devised, hidden from everyone, so that the chariot would suddenly jam while running. The chariot festival was held with great splendour. But on the festival day, after the chariot had moved a short distance down the street, it stopped. Then those same astrologers sent by Chellaatha Gounder appeared again. They frightened everyone by saying some offence against the Goddess had occurred and that this was why the chariot would not move and had become stuck. Not only that—they threatened that unless the chariot ran its full course and came to rest properly, danger would befall the people of the whole village. Their supposed remedy was that some married woman must place her head beneath the chariot wheel and offer herself as a sacrifice; otherwise, they said, the entire village would be destroyed. It was an outrageous lie they poured forth. Saying that it did not matter if their own family was destroyed, did not matter if she herself died, but no danger must come to the village people who depended on them, Thamarai Naachchiyar prepared to offer herself beneath the chariot wheel. Kundrudaiyaan said, ‘If you are going, what am I to do alone? Let us both go together,’ and prepared to lie under the chariot wheel with Thamarai and die. The whole village rushed to stop them, but they would not listen. It never occurred to them that the Goddess described as the form of love and the embodiment of grace would not destroy her own people. They believed the astrology. They were caught in Chellaatha Gounder's net of conspiracy. Defying the villagers, they lay beneath the chariot wheels and ordered that the chariot be pulled over them. At that moment Raakkiyannan arrived. He understood Chellaatha Gounder's plot. Looking at Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchi, he said, ‘First both of you get up from beneath the chariot. I will make this chariot run without any sacrifice at all.’

<!-- source boundary: scan 185 → scan 186 -->

“He did exactly as he said. He found and corrected the sabotage done to the chariot—and then the chariot moved.

“‘Stop wandering from sacred place to sacred place in the name of temple, tank, chariot, festival, *archana* and prayer. Stay at the Kudaiyur mansion, or stay at the palace in Sankaranmalai. Do not place upon God the blame for the growth of a family that can arise through the union of man and woman,’ Raakkiyannan said with a laugh. Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchiyar still felt some doubt. Later they themselves thought it over. They came and stayed at the palace in Sankaranmalai. The hardships of the early days of marriage had passed, and through Thamarai Naachchiyar's conduct in keeping with the Kural words ‘guarding herself and caring for the man who made her his own,’ their prosperous life had grown. They did not wish to waste it. Sankaranmalai had once been a very cradle of nature. Beside waterfalls that rushed with a murmur and raised waves of feeling in young hearts, there were countless scenes of peacocks dancing, cuckoos singing and deer bounding, all joined in delight. Countless! Though there is not so much of it today, twenty years ago Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchiyar were enchanted by those scenes. With them now seated before us as people blessed with children, it would not be fitting to say—or to describe further—that it was through Sankaranmalai's showers of natural delight that they came to understand the sweetness of married life. As a continuation of that understanding, Thamarai Naachchiyar became pregnant, and Kundrudaiyaan was overjoyed. He held a celebration at Kudaiyur.

“Sankaranmalai too took on the appearance of a festival. News of Thamarai Naachchiyar's pregnancy was, as customary, sent to Aarichampatti as well. Though Thamarai had no desire to send word, Kundrudaiyaan, who did not keep things brooding in his mind, properly conveyed the news to the related—

<!-- source boundary: scan 186 → scan 187 -->

—household. But according to the custom of the Kongu Vellalar clan, in the seventh month of pregnancy, the woman's natal family should come with food, sweets and various kinds of packed rice, hold a grand feast and take the woman back to her mother's home. Kundrudaiyaan expected you people of Aarichampatti to come, and all he received was disappointment.”

As Maayavar said this and looked intently at Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, the latter lowered his head, thinking of his conduct in the past.

“If one regrets a mistake even after much time has passed, that deserves appreciation,” Maayavar said with delight, and continued his explanation.

“Would the news that Thamarai was seven months pregnant have failed to reach Chellaatha Gounder in Valanaadu? Chellaatha Gounder became afraid that if a child were born to Kundrudaiyaan, it might one day raise a claim to rights in Valanaadu. Kundrudaiyaan himself, though he had not received all the land that should have come to him, did not trouble himself about it; he governed as a guardian with what he had. But who could predict that the children born to him would be as guided by conscience as he was? So Chellaatha Gounder decided that Kundrudaiyaan's line must not increase, because it might become a great enemy to him in the future. Two midwives were prepared to attend Thamarai Naachchiyar's delivery. Chellaatha Gounder and Maanthiyappan gave those midwives many instructions and sent them away with a purse of gold as reward. Thamarai's time for childbirth arrived. As she cried out unable to bear the pain, Kundrudaiyaan watched his wife's suffering, not knowing what to do, and wilted in distress. At that moment word came that two midwives had arrived. Seeing his wife's agony, he thought God himself must have sent the midwives—

<!-- source boundary: scan 187 → scan 188 -->

—to him. But what matters is the plan of Chellaatha Gounder, who had actually sent them. They had already been instructed in the conspiracy and told exactly what to do.

“Though Thamarai Naachchiyar would cry out under labour pain raging like a storm, once the child was born she would become calm and lie utterly exhausted. In those few seconds they were to hide Thamarai's baby. Kundrudaiyaan would run in to see the child. They were to tell him that no child had been born, that only blood had poured from the womb, and that the tragedy had occurred because the baby had disintegrated within the womb.

“With this plan, the two midwives entered the delivery room. Thamarai Naachchiyar screamed, unable to bear the pain. Though the women of the palace tried anxiously to enter the room, the midwives sternly ordered all of them to remain far away. Kundrudaiyaan, distraught, kept circling the delivery room. Looking at him, the midwives said humbly, ‘If you want us to do our work properly and for Amma to have a safe delivery, please go and sit in your place. Otherwise, we will leave. Forgive us for speaking this way. We say it only because we must perform our duty correctly without disturbance.’ Kundrudaiyaan replied, ‘All right, all right! Somehow, let Thamarai have a safe delivery. I leave the burden upon you and upon God,’ and withdrew, wiping his troubled eyes.

After Thamarai Naachchiyar had suffered labour pain for a long time, a child was born in one final peak of pain. A boy! Thamarai lay exhausted and unconscious. The older midwife lifted the child and handed him to the younger—

<!-- source boundary: scan 188 → scan 189 -->

—midwife, telling her to hide him and run. She took the infant and fled. Not long after the younger midwife ran away, a surprise awaited the elder one.

Thamarai Naachchiyar began to suffer labour pains again. Then another boy was born. The elder midwife picked him up too and ran along the same path taken by the younger one.

A figure who had been waiting for them, wrapped in a black cloak, took the two children into his hands.

Exhausted from giving birth to two children, Thamarai Naachchiyar lay without yet fully regaining consciousness. Wondering why no sound could be heard from the delivery room, Kundrudaiyaan slowly came and peered in. Thamarai Naachchiyar had just opened her eyes. Seeing no child beside her, she began to cry out, “My child? My child? Where is my child?” Kundrudaiyaan understood nothing. By then the palace women had gathered there, and he shouted furiously at them, “Where is the child? Where are those midwives?” Guessing that something had happened to the baby, Thamarai sobbed, “What have you done to my child?”

“Not one child, Amma! Two children were born—two lion cubs!” The figure in the black cloak came and stood at the doorway of the delivery room, carrying both babies in his arms. Beside him stood the two midwives, bound in shackles.

“What is all this?” Kundrudaiyaan cried in alarm.

“A plot to kill the children! A plot to say no child was born! I have smashed that plot to dust. I have arrested the conspirators too!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 189 → scan 190 -->

Thus the dark figure answered majestically.

Thamarai cast aside somewhere all the exhaustion of childbirth, ran forward and took the babies into her arms.

The dark figure removed the black cloak that had hidden him—it was Raakkiyannan beneath it.

Kundrudaiyaan, beside himself with emotion, embraced Raakkiyannan tightly.”

As Maayavar described this event, tears streamed from the eyes of Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchiyar. The others let out long sighs. Maayavar continued.

“I have told you up to the point where Raakkiyannan saved the children who were about to be made to disappear. Now I shall tell you how those children disappeared again,” he began.
