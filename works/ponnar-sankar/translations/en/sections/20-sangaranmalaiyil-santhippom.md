17

Shall We Meet at Sankaranmalai?

They could not have travelled even one *kal* from Raachchaandaar Malai before the horses of Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai drew ahead of the chariot driven by Vaiyam Perumaan.

“Let the chariot and the warriors follow along the same path we take!” Ponnar said in a commanding tone.

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu pulled slightly on his horse's reins and asked hesitantly, “But this is a forest path, is it not?”

“It is a forest path! We will have to struggle a little to travel through it. And it is not merely a forest path; it is rough and uneven too! The enemies may follow us thinking we are going to Aarichampatti. Changing our route is what suits this situation,” Ponnar said.

“Then where are we going?” Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu asked, surprise showing in his eyes.

“To Sankaranmalai!” Ponnar replied.

“To Sankaranmalai?” There was meaning in the question Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu raised.

Sankaranmalai was one of the regions under the authority of Kundrudaiyaan, lord of many hills.

<!-- source boundary: scan 164 → scan 165 -->

The bitterness between Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu and Kundrudaiyaan over the marriage of his younger sister Thamarai Naachchi had still not disappeared, and so Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu hesitated over how he could go to Sankaranmalai, which belonged to Kundrudaiyaan.

He himself felt ashamed to explain that old incident to those brave young men now.

The continuing hatred Chellaatha Gounder and his son Maanthiyappan had earned among the people, and the inhuman way their misdeeds kept revealing themselves, had created such a dreadful image of them in Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's mind that he sometimes thought that, somehow, the sudden turn in his sister Thamarai's wedding that day had ended well as far as she was concerned. Even so, the shock of his younger sister instantly casting aside the family into which she had been born and raised and setting out to live a married life with Nelliyangodan had never completely left his heart.

He did not feel inclined to answer Ponnar with a contrary word. Out of the respect he felt for those valiant young men who had saved his entire family from danger after sudden danger, and from the depth of his affection for them, he accepted Ponnar's idea exactly as it was and followed, together with the Aarichampatti warriors.

The forest path was thick with closely packed trees. Along that road leading north from Raachchaandaar Malai, Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai rode ahead on horseback; Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's horse went close beside them; behind came the chariot, with warriors surrounding it. Thus began their swift journey.

After they had gone a *kal* or two, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, still on horseback, looked toward Ponnar and Sankar and—

<!-- source boundary: scan 165 → scan 166 -->

—asked, “Do you already know anyone at Sankaranmalai?”

Ponnar answered. “Our teacher Raakkiyannan has taken us to that place once or twice. He told us it belongs to Kundrudaiyaan, who is also called Nelliyangodan. It seems that Kundrudaiyaan sometimes comes to Sankaran Malai and stays in the mansion there. Our teacher himself has taken us there, saying that Kundrudaiyaan gave Raakkiyannan permission to come to that mansion whenever he wished and stay as many days as he wished. So it is on the basis of that past experience that we decided it would be best for us to go there now. Once we are staying in the Sankaranmalai mansion, the enemies will not be able to enter so easily. The fortification around the mansion and the moat around the fortification are capable of breaking the enemy's strength.”

Relieved by Ponnar's answer, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu raised another doubt.

“If we suddenly enter Sankaranmalai Fort with an army, will the commanders or warriors there allow us in?”

“There is no need to worry about that at all! I already told you that it is a place familiar to us. We can station our Aarichampatti warriors around the fort, and if we explain the situation to the guards there, they will not merely let us stay—they will welcome and receive us.”

Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's face showed that he was still not completely satisfied. Understanding this, Sankar spoke to him.

“We could even have gone straight from Raachchaandaar Malai to Aarichampatti. The Raachchaandaar Malai warriors following us will head toward Aarichampatti. Once those who fled have gone, the men who remain will travel some distance along the road to Aarichampatti—

<!-- source boundary: scan 166 → scan 167 -->

—and by the time they become bewildered at not finding us and choose another route, we can already have reached Sankaranmalai.”

Though Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu inwardly admired the strategy in Sankar's explanation, doubt after doubt still rose and crashed within him like waves.

Maanthiyappan, who had fled after being struck down, would go to Chellaatha Gounder and tell him what had happened. Chellaatha Gounder would take his son and run to Thalaiyur Kaali. There was a major event certain to inflame King Thalaiyur Kaali's anger: the killing of Thirumalai, one of his commanders. In any case, within a few days Thalaiyur Kaali's army might march toward Aarichampatti. Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's heart pounded at the thought that this might happen; yet at the same time the resolve that, to preserve honour, he would have to face whatever came had driven down and taken root in him like an iron stake.

The eyes of Muthayi and Pavalayi, seated in the chariot, clustered around Ponnar and Sankar, who rode proudly on horseback ahead of their vehicle.

Though the disaster on the road had created a situation in which their very lives might be ruined and the whole Aarichampatti family might be destroyed, that same disaster had also given them the chance to feast their eyes once again on the warriors who had come to save them from it. Thinking of this, the eyes of the two young women shone.

Those treasures of beauty had thought of the two young men as men who, through bodily strength and extraordinary courage, had faced the whirlwind, brought down the mountain snake and overcome the crocodile. Now, learning that the same two young men had also proved victorious warriors in battle against a great army—

<!-- source boundary: scan 167 → scan 168 -->

—they were entranced and found pleasure in that too.

Muthayi feasted her eyes on Ponnar and imagined seating him upon the throne of her heart, resting her face upon his knee and caressing his valour-filled chest with her flower-soft hand, as though delighting in the experience. Then, fearing that her younger sister Pavalayi might notice her, she glanced sideways at her, felt shy at herself and lowered her head.

Pavalayi, having surrendered herself to the beauty of Sankar's moustache, wandered in a dream-world in which she sat beside him on that same horse and encircled his teakwood-like body with her arms. Afraid that her elder sister might see her and laugh, she bent her head aside in shy embarrassment.

Riding ahead of the chariot, Ponnar was angry with God. If only he had been created with one eye on his face and another at the back of his head, could he not travel while watching the road with one eye and the maiden in the chariot with the other?

Such was his fantasy. And what of Sankar? What occurred to him was no ordinary fantasy either. He wished that he could raise his palm and look at it, and that it would instantly turn into a mirror in which Pavalayi's face in the chariot would appear. Then he too could travel while looking into that mirror.

Before the army had set out for Raachchaandaar Malai, the announcement Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu had made at the entrance of Aarichampatti Fort was still swinging like a golden cradle in their hearts.

“If these warriors pluck and bring me the fruit of victory, I shall give them the fruits I have borne,”—

<!-- source boundary: scan 168 → scan 169 -->

—the lion-like young men kept recalling his thundered words, soaking their hearts in a spring of delight.

There—the top of a temple tower came into view as though announcing that Sankaranmalai Fort was close by.

Without knowing who was inside the fort, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, his wife and children, and Ponnar, Sankar and Veeramalai who accompanied them, were approaching.

Kundrudaiyaan had accepted Maayavar's suggestion that they could go from the Chellandiyamman temple hall to Kudaiyur and discuss everything in detail at Kundrudaiya Gounder's palace. Even so, before getting into the carriage, Kundrudaiyaan offered Maayavar an alternative idea.

“Maayavar! You are going to tell us good news about our children who disappeared. You say you will come all the way to our mansion for that. But one thing: come with us to the very place where our children disappeared. If you tell us the joyful news of where those missing treasures are at the very place where they vanished, then in the very place where we lost our children and cried out in grief, we can hear from your own mouth that they are alive and feel our hearts fill with joy.”

“Where are you calling me, Nelliyangoda?”

“Nowhere else, Maayavar. I am inviting you only to Sankaran Malai, which belongs to me.”

“Are you hesitating because you think going to the Kudaiyur mansion will cause you some trouble on my account?”

“Che! Che! Nothing like that. From here Sankaranmalai is nearer than Kudaiyur. I also wished that you might see Sankaranmalai. The arrangement of the mansion there, the strength of the fortification and the sight of the moat will greatly—

<!-- source boundary: scan 169 → scan 170 -->

—appeal to you. If you do not like my suggestion, we can go to Kudaiyur itself; I have no objection at all.”

Maayavar interrupted Kundrudaiyaan. “No! No! There is no need, no need! Let us go to the Sankaranmalai mansion itself. Since you say that is where the children disappeared, I too am eager to see the place. Let us go there.”

Rather than travel southwest from the Chellandiyamman temple to Kudaiyur on the southern bank of the Amaravathi River, one could turn a little southeast from the temple, pass Madhukkarai and reach Sankaran Malai much sooner. As soon as Maayavar expressed his wish to go there, Kundrudaiyaan looked at Thamarai Naachchiyar and Arukkaani Thangam and signalled with his eyes for them to get into the carriage.

Although Thamarai Naachchiyar had come to Sankaranmalai five or six times with her husband, each time she came, the memory of her children having disappeared from the mansion in that village had tormented her deeply. A mother's heart might always lament for lost children, but when she came to the place where she had lost them, did she not suffer as though looking upon tombs built for those children? Because of this, on some occasions when her husband invited her, Thamarai had avoided coming to Sankaranmalai. But now she liked her husband's idea very much.

Maayavar had hinted that the treasures lost long ago were alive. He was going to tell them the complete news of where they were and how they were—and he was going to tell it at the same Sankaranmalai where those treasures had slipped from her hands. At that thought, her heart had grown heavy—

<!-- source boundary: scan 170 → scan 171 -->

—with unbearable joy. Arukkaani Thangam too had learned, from the writing made by her mother's unending tears of sorrow, that her two elder brothers had disappeared in childhood. She therefore became excited and regarded Maayavar's coming with them to Sankaranmalai, apparently with important information, as a great opportunity.

Kundrudaiyaan, Thamarai Naachchiyar and Arukkaani Thangam took their seats in the carriage from the Kudaiyur mansion. Maayavar travelled on horseback beside it, and several guards followed on horses. That very night they reached the Sankaranmalai mansion.

Knowing nothing of their presence inside the mansion, Ponnar and Sankar brought Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu, the Aarichampatti family, and the warriors into the entrance of that settlement.

Seeing more movement of warriors and more bustle among the guards than usual at the entrance of the Sankaranmalai mansion, Ponnar and Sankar turned to look at Veeramalai Sambuvan.

“Wait a little while. I will go inside, find out who has arrived, and come back.”

Having said this, Veeramalai Sambuvan urged his horse toward the gate of Sankaranmalai Fort.

The Sankaranmalai warriors at the gate recognised Veeramalai Sambuvan. They bowed their heads in greeting and not only allowed him to enter the fort, but accompanied him inside.

Outside the fort, beneath the shade of the trees, Ponnar, Sankar and Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu waited on horseback. The chariot carrying Muthayi and Pavalayi had been stopped beneath the shade of a neem tree. Vaiyam Perumaan had—

<!-- source boundary: scan 171 → scan 172 -->

—got down from the chariot and was walking here and there nearby. The Aarichampatti soldiers sat in a line on their horses some distance away.

“We too are the border-guarding rulers of a region, yet here we are, with our warriors, having to wait so long at the fort gate of another border-guarding ruler like ourselves!” Silambayi complained inwardly as she reclined in the chariot. While they had been confined inside Raachchaandaar Malai Fort, her worry had been, “Will we escape alive?” Now it was a matter of dignity: “Must we wait outside for this long?”

There had indeed been some delay, but at last Veeramalai Sambuvan came slowly out of the fort on horseback, smiling as though that smile were the sign of a sweet feeling overflowing within him. As he came near, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu looked at him and asked eagerly, “What happened?”

“Kundrudaiyaan, his wife and his daughter are staying inside the fort.” The moment this answer came from Veeramalai, Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu started and, without realising it, cried out rather loudly, “Ah!”
