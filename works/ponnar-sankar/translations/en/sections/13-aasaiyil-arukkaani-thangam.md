11

Aasaiyil Arukkaani Thangam

The chariot bound for Aarichampatti sped away from the Chellandiyamman temple festival pavilion. Vaiyam Perumaan sat on the front seat. In the next row sat Silambayi, with Muthayi and Pavalayi on either side of her. To reach the Maniyangurichi region called Aarichampatti, they had to travel along the rough road beside the Kaveri, pass through Kulithalai and then head southeast.

Though Thamarai Naachchiyar could not come close to see her elder brother's children off, she remained inside the festival pavilion, blessing them in her heart and watching the chariot go. Muthayi and Pavalayi, those epics of beauty who seemed like paintings brought to life, would not leave Thamarai Naachchiyar's eyes. Even after the chariot had gone far beyond the pavilion, the faces of those young parrots seemed to appear before her, scattering clustered smiles like smiles made of Korkai pearls.

The scene on the screen of Thamarai's mind seemed to polish the brightness of her face. Yet, like sudden lightning, a streak of pain tore through that radiance. The heated exchange that had taken place between her and her elder brother at Aarichampatti when she married Nelliyangodan floated up and gathered in the waves of her memory.

‘Anna! Before you, my own brother, I swear upon the mother who bore me—upon my father

too. You said that family affection ended today. It will not! It can never end! Anna, you mocked me by saying there was no house or threshold where ceremonial gifts could be brought. You are going to see me and my husband living in a grand mansion! My dear brother who said the bonds of family had burned away—you will come and beg me to give your daughter in marriage to the lion-cub son I shall bear!’

As soon as Thamarai had thundered those words,

‘If you are going to bear one, then bear two lion cubs! I will bring both of them and tie them like dogs at the entrance of my house to stand guard,’ Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu had replied mockingly.

‘I will bear them, Anna, I will! And I shall live to enjoy the sight of you bearing two daughters and coming in search of those lions!’ Thamarai Naachchiyar had cried out. To her, all of it now seemed as though it were happening at that very moment.

In that feeling she stood as an image of sorrow. Just as she had vowed that day, she bore two lion cubs. But within two years of those lion cubs being born and growing, she and her beloved husband Kundrudaiyaan had no idea where they had gone. There was no deity they had not implored, no temple they had not worshipped at, no sacred waters in which they had not bathed.

From the moment she saw her elder brother Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's two daughters, Thamarai Naachchi's longing grew stronger.

“Just as I said that day, Anna too has now had two daughters. But the sons I bore are not here for Anna to come and plead that they should marry those girls! Amma, Mother! For how many years have we prayed to you? Has your heart still not softened?” Thamarai wailed aloud as she stood worshipping Chellandiyamman.
