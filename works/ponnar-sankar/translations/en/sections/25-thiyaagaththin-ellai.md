22

The Limit of Sacrifice

“Veeramalai, released through Thamarai Naachchiyar's compassion, leapt onto his horse and left Sankaran Malai. He reached Maarik Goundan Paalayam so swiftly that he himself wondered how he had come so fast. Firmly believing that Raakkiyannan would be at the camp with the children, he drove his horse toward it. As he entered, he looked around the camp entrance and interior to see whether any Thalaiyur warriors were visible. Seeing none, Veeramalai wondered whether the Thalaiyur Kaali warriors who had been waiting to receive the children from Raakkiyannan might already have taken them away. Che! At once, the light of his faith that his teacher would have done no such thing appeared and drove away the darkness of that doubt.

He dismounted, left the horse standing there and went into the camp. His teacher was not there. Sweat poured from Veeramalai. Could Raakkiyannan have gone straight from Sankaranmalai Fort to Thalaiyur? The thought made Veeramalai tremble. His step showed a weariness entirely unlike his youth. Yet he mastered himself and led the horse he had ridden toward the camp stable to tie it there. The sight he found brought a sigh of relief.

The horse Raakkiyannan had ridden to Sankaranmalai Fort was tied in the stable. Then the teacher must—

<!-- source boundary: scan 208 → scan 209 -->

—have gone home, Veeramalai decided, and he hurried there half running, half walking.

The teacher's house door was bolted from inside. Of the windows set beside the high raised platforms on either side of the entrance threshold, one alone stood slightly open, like an eyelid left ajar while someone slept. In that tense situation, Veeramalai felt there was nothing wrong in hiding and watching. He climbed quietly onto the platform and looked inside through the narrow gap in the window.

Azhagu Naachchiyar and Raakkiyannan were sitting on the cot. Two children lay in Azhagu Naachchiyar's lap. Two children lay in Raakkiyannan's lap. Their conversation reached Veeramalai's ears.

“Azhagu Naachchi! When our twins were born, even I was shocked by the news at first. Only slowly did that shock blossom into happiness. Now, as far as I am concerned, that happiness has multiplied many times. But I cannot understand how you feel.”

“Your happiness is my happiness too. Even so, what you have done has created some confusion in my mind. Who are these two children? Where have you brought them from? Why?”

“Would I keep it from you? I am going to tell you. I must tell you.”

“Since you came, you have only spoken around the matter without giving me any details. That is precisely why I am afraid.”

“I will tell you, Azhagu! I went to the naming ceremony of Kundrudaiyaan's children. These are those very children.”

“What are you saying? Did you ask the palace and carry them away with their consent? Or... or...?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 209 → scan 210 -->

“You mean to ask whether I stole the children and brought them here. You need not struggle over how to ask it. I will tell you myself: yes, I stole them.”

“Stole them? Why? Do not torment me any longer. Tell me quickly!”

“If I had not stolen these children away, then through Chellaatha Gounder's conspiracy they would somehow have been handed over to King Thalaiyur Kaali. Chellaatha Gounder is desperate to end the lives of these tender buds right now.”

“Why? Why such treachery? He has seized Kundrudaiyaan's Valanaadu and rules it. Is that not enough? Why must he kill Kundrudaiyaan's children?”

“He wants the rule of Valanaadu gained through deceit to be governed by himself, and after him only by his own descendants. He wants no one from Kundrudaiyaan's family to rise and recover Valanaadu. That is why such a noble thought has dawned upon him! Doubting whether he could do it himself, he deceived and frightened Thalaiyur Kaali and devised a plan to have him destroy these children. Fortunately, I was in Thalaiyur at the time, and I asked the King of Thalaiyur to leave the responsibility of bringing the children to me. Now these children are safe in my hands.”

“Will the King of Thalaiyur not ask you for the children?”

“He certainly will. Before he asks, I myself must take them and give them to him.”

“If you hand them over, what will become of these children?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 210 → scan 211 -->

“They may be killed according to Chellaatha Gounder's plan.”

“What are you saying? Will you too become an accomplice to killing these children who look like golden icons?”

“I do not wish to be an accomplice! Azhagu Naachchi, it is not merely that they are Kundrudaiyaan's children. When I remember that they are the grandchildren of Koalatha Gounder, I feel as though my heart will split. Koalatha Gounder ruled as a mountain of noble qualities. The lamp of his lineage, Pavalathaall, was revered by all the people as a mother. Koalatha Gounder was famed not only for building many temples and conducting *kudamuzhukku*, but also for establishing land rights for the great clans. Many good people, called Vaangalaan and Paattappan, praised and blessed him openly and wholeheartedly. After ruling proudly in many directions, the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings had been reduced to narrower territories. When a boundary dispute among them grew into a great trouble, war broke out. Koalatha Gounder appealed to stop the war from continuing, brought all three kings to the Madhukkarai Chellandiyamman temple, negotiated peace and settled the boundary dispute among them. Delighted by that settlement, the Chola king gave Koalatha Gounder a large tract of land near Thirukkaampuliyur as a gift. With the friendship of the Chola king and the respect of the Chera and Pandya kings, Koalatha Gounder obtained the right to guard and govern a vast territory and laboured among the people as one of them. Why, even this large house in which we now sit and talk, the gardens, water sources and lands around it, our camp and the groves attached to it—all were gifts given to our family in those days by Koalatha Gounder.

<!-- source boundary: scan 211 → scan 212 -->

“How can my heart permit me to carry the children of the open-hearted Kundrudaiyaan, born into such a broad-minded and generous family, and hand them over to a slaughterhouse? I cannot. I certainly cannot.”

“Then are you going to refuse to hand these children over to the King of Thalaiyur?”

“If I do not hand them over, what answer will I give him? Was it not I who accepted responsibility for bringing these children?”

“Then tell him you abducted them and killed them.”

“It was precisely because that could not be believed that they made it clear at the time that the children must be brought to Thalaiyur and handed over.”

“There was no need for you to force such an unjust burden upon your own head.”

“If I had not agreed to do this, the King of Thalaiyur's men would somehow have killed the children.”

“Have you decided that they must not kill them, so we ourselves will?”

“If I had decided that, why would I have been talking with you all this time?”

“What are you saying? If the children must be handed to the King of Thalaiyur, but must not die, how can that be possible? Have you forgotten that you are not placing the children in Thalaiyur's hands, but at the edge of the heavy sword?”

“I must hand children over to the King of Thalaiyur. But not these treasures named Ponnar and Sankar, born to Kundrudaiyaan.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 212 → scan 213 -->

As he said this, Veeramalai, watching through the window, noticed a tremor appear in his teacher's voice. Veeramalai's eagerness crossed all bounds. Placing her hand on her husband's shoulder, Azhagu Naachchiyar asked:

“Then which treasures are you going to hand over?”

“Azhagu Naachchi! Prepare your heart to bear what I am about to say. For I am going to say something no mother could bear.”

As the teacher spoke these words, tears fell in streams from his eyes. Azhagu Naachchiyar started up and clasped the children tightly to her chest. “What is it you are going to say?” she asked. Teacher Raakkiyannan stood speechless for a moment. Then, holding the children, he paced along the edge of the cot with his head bowed. Afterwards he laid the children in his hands upon the cot. Then he went near Azhagu Naachchiyar, stretched out both hands and asked for the children she held.

At the sight of Azhagu Naachchiyar pressing those children to the womb that had formed and nurtured them and the breasts that had fed them, while tears poured down, Veeramalai felt as though the blood in his own body might stop flowing.

“Come, give them,” the husband said, stretching out both hands. But his wife Azhagu Naachchiyar stood hesitating, shaking her head as though to say, “No, no!”

“Azhagu Naachchi! Not for my sake. For the future of our Perungkudi people, you and I must make this sacrifice. As repayment of our debt of gratitude for the countless benefits our people and country received from Koalatha Gounder, we must save his grandchildren. We ourselves once wondered why twins had been born to us. But today? Look how great a good it has become! We can hand over our own two children to the Thalaiyur man as though they were Kundrudaiyaan's twins, can we not? We can protect Koalatha Gounder's two tender flowers from being crushed, can we not?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 213 → scan 214 -->

“Ayyo! Only now do I understand the pain of twins having been born to us! If only one child had been born, such an idea would never have sprouted in your mind!”

Azhagu Naachchiyar began to sob. Comforting her, the teacher took the children from her hands into his own.

“Azhagu Naachchi! Do not cry. This father's pain is no less than a mother's pain. But for the future wellbeing of our Perungkudi community—for the valour and dignity of our clan to gain the glory of raising its banner to the sky—we two must make this sacrifice.”

“Ayyo! How can a mother willingly dare to lose her own children? You tell me!”

“She cannot. But there is no other way. Azhagu Naachchi! Losing what can be lost is not sacrifice. Some people, out of selfishness, will not even dare to lose what can be lost. A chance to make the sacrifice of losing the great wealth that must not be lost—the wealth of one's children—for the future of this soil comes only rarely. We must not let that chance pass and become selfish people. Please listen to me. From now on, the little treasures lying on this cot—Ponnar and Sankar—are our children. It is our duty to raise them into capable men and, at the proper time, hand them over to Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchi. Until then, no one must know that these children are Ponnar and Sankar. Here—give these children one final kiss and send them on their way. Let us forget that two such children were ever born to us. Do not be shaken. Do not cry. If it is true that you are the wife of a warrior; if it is true that even as a mother you are selfless; if it is true that the wellbeing and future light of our Perungkudi community are our goal—then give me leave!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 214 → scan 215 -->

Azhagu Naachchiyar, stunned, took her own children from her husband's hands and, until her longing was spent, showered them with kisses—one or two, a hundred, two hundred, a thousand, a hundred thousand. Then she collapsed in a faint upon the cot, buried her head at the feet of Kundrudaiyaan's two children and sobbed.

Holding his own two children in his arms, the teacher turned toward the doorway.

“Veeramalai! I know you have been watching everything. I deliberately left the window slightly open so that you would watch. You are the witness to everything that happened. Everything I told Azhagu Naachchi applies to you too. Like me, you too are loyal to Kundrudaiyaan's family. I am going to Thalaiyur with these children. You look after Azhagu Naachchiyar.”

Saying this, he went outside.

Veeramalai's eyes were now waterfalls as he finished relating these details with emotion.
