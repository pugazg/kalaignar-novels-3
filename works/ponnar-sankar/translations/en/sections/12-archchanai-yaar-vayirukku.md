10

Archana—For Whose Stomach?

Muthayi and Pavalayi had been fasting since morning in order to fulfil their prayer by taking part in the karagam-releasing festival. Added to that was the suffering they had endured in the river because of the whirlwind, and they were utterly exhausted.

Silambayi fully believed that it was only through the Amman's grace that her beloved daughters had escaped alive. She went around the sanctum of Chellandiyamman and fell in worship as tears of joy overflowed.

She had come to the karagam festival to pray that both her dear daughters should have pleasant and prosperous lives. Silambayi now rejoiced in the thought that the terrible danger hanging over them had passed.

Silambayi, Muthayi and Pavalayi lit a *maavilakku* and stood with their eyes closed before the principal image of Chellandiyamman.

The calm that came after the storm had settled not only on the bank of the Kaveri but also within their hearts. Worshipping the Amman in the gentle comfort that calm brought was soothing to them.

Many of those who had come to see the festival gathered family by family, lit *maavilakku* lamps here and there, and worshipped before the Amman shrine in absorbed devotion.

Because they were women of the Aarichampatti-Maniyangurichi household, the temple priest approached Silambayi with great respect, greeted her and said, “Ammani! If you wish to have any *archana* performed for the Amman, this servant is ready.”

“We have brought everything needed for the *archana*,” Silambayi replied with a smile, pointing to a silver basket beside her.

“Is that so?” the priest asked with even greater deference as he took the silver basket in his hands. It contained four or five coconuts, plenty of betel leaves and areca nut, flower garlands and fruit.

“Ammani! In whose names shall the *archana* be performed?” the priest asked respectfully. Silambayi said, “For both my daughters, Muthayi and Pavalayi—and for Vaiyam Perumaan, my son as well.”

Vaiyam Perumaan was not there at that moment. He was near the chariot, seeing that the horses were fed and patting them.

Before Silambayi had finished saying that the *archana* should be performed in the names of her daughters and son, Muthayi interrupted.

“Amma, the young man who saved me—could we not have an *archana* to the Amman performed in his name too?”

A question mark of surprise flashed across Silambayi's face. Still, she calmed herself. Certain that her daughter could not have said such a thing with any wrong intention, and thinking that it was only proper to show gratitude to that young man, she looked at Pavalayi and asked with a smile, “Did you hear what your elder sister said?” Pavalayi, who had wanted to say the same thing herself, replied, “Yes, Amma! I too

was thinking of saying it. The one who kept my boat from capsizing and was bitten by a crocodile for my sake—we should have an *archana* performed in his name too, Amma!”

Silambayi's eyes seemed to bulge beyond her eyelids and dart this way and that like the heads of chicks inside a nest watching for their mother bird's return. Yet her face did not redden. She pressed her lips with her teeth and looked at both girls, but there was no sweep of anger in that gaze.

Thoughts also brushed Silambayi's heart: why should she not believe that both the treasures she had borne were asking for *archana* in those young men's names purely out of gratitude?

“Ayyo! We do not know those young men's names!” she said, looking at her daughters. “Appa or someone asked them... we do not know what they replied,” both answered at once. “Could you not have listened properly and learned what their names were? Did you not even have that much concern?” Silambayi chided them affectionately. “Amma, Anna was there then—if we ask him, he will know!” Muthayi suggested excitedly.

By then Pavalayi ran and stopped a short distance from the chariot and called loudly, “Anna! Amma is calling you urgently!”

Vaiyam Perumaan, who had been patting the horses and preparing them for the journey, heard his younger sister say that their mother was calling and hurried into the temple.

“What is it, Amma? Why did you call me?”

“Nothing! Those young men saved your sisters from danger. So that they may live

well, I wanted to have an *archana* performed for the Amman in their names.”

Even as their mother said this to her son, Muthayi's and Pavalayi's eyes became like tiny narrowed bees embracing one another and humming. Their eyes seemed to embrace, saying to each other, “See how the loving bond of the affection that bore us protects its treasures.” With that feeling their cheeks took on the red colour of mango fruit. The sweet breath that escaped as a deep sigh made the breasts of those young statues rise and seemed to stage a contest of beauty on the Kaveri bank.

Hidden from them, Maanthiyappan staggered in intoxication at the captivating beauty of those living portraits. Those queens of beauty were anxious that their “chosen deities” should accept them and were ready to offer themselves as *naivedyam*; he writhed like a mule eager to crush, chew and devour flowers that had bloomed for a temple.

“Thambi, do you know the names of those young men?”

“I do not, Amma!”

“When they saved your sisters, someone on the bank asked their names. I did not notice what they said. These two did not notice either. Did you not hear?”

“No, Amma!”

“Then how are we to perform an *archana* to the Amman in those young men's names?”

“We can simply perform the *archana* in the name of ‘the young men’!” Vaiyam Perumaan said with a laugh.

“How can that work, son?” his mother hesitated.

“There are two young men. We can think of the elder as Periyaandaan and the younger as Chinnaandaan, names of our own choosing, and perform the *archana* that way!”

“What are you saying, Thambi? How can we invent names ourselves? Surely the names their father and mother gave them are the names that stand!”

“What can we do if we do not know those names, Amma? Either we perform the *archana* under the names I suggested, or we leave the *archana* undone.”

When Vaiyam Perumaan stated such a conclusion, the faces of Muthayi and Pavalayi changed. Pavalayi alone managed to compose herself. “Anna! Amma has already wished for it. So will you find some way for us to perform the *archana*? If you simply say there should be no *archana* at all, how much will Amma be disappointed?” she said, glancing sideways at her mother's face.

Silambayi now had a fine opportunity to understand her daughters' hearts very well. Even so, she kept the truth she had understood hidden like a fruit behind leaves and looked mischievously at Pavalayi.

“I am not disappointed at all. If we do not know the names, what can we do? All right, priest! Complete the *archana* in the names of my daughters and my son,” Silambayi said. But Vaiyam Perumaan did not agree.

“Wait a little, Amma... Look there—Thanakkodi Chettiar of Aadhisettip Paalayam and the Kundrudaiyaan family are lighting *maavilakku* for the Amman. I will go and ask whether they know the names of those young men,” he said and started to leave. At once the startled

Silambayi caught hold of him and cried, “Wait! Wait!”

“Why? What is it, Amma?” Vaiyam Perumaan asked in surprise. Silambayi explained her agitation.

“You casually said ‘the Kundrudaiyaan family’!”

“No, Amma! I know that he too is a major landholder. He has even come to this festival with all that distinction. I did not speak of him as though he were an ordinary man.”

“That is not what I mean! You know only that they are the Kundrudaiyaan family. You do not know who that Kundrudaiyaan actually is, do you?”

“Who is he, Amma?”

“Do you know Nelliyangodan, husband of your aunt Thamarai Naachchiyar?”

“I have heard of him. I have heard the whole story of how Aunt and that uncle were driven out of our house, Amma!”

“You have heard of them. Have you ever seen them?”

“Appa often says that there is no relationship between them and us even in death or life. If that is so, how could I have seen them, Amma?”

“You have just seen them now!”

“What are you saying, Amma?”

“Your uncle Nelliyangodan is now Kundrudaiyaan. The lamp of the family beside Kundrudaiyaan is your aunt Thamarai Naachchiyar.”

On hearing this, not only Vaiyam Perumaan but Muthayi and Pavalayi too were plunged into amazement.

“They say ‘close relations’; now relations that had gone far away have come close again, Amma!” Vaiyam Perumaan said, his face blossoming with happiness as he looked at his mother.

“Thambi! Go and see them if you wish, under the pretext of asking whether they know the young men's names. Please do not speak of kinship and bring your father's anger upon yourself. Your father will not hate only you; he will hate me too. Great trouble will come upon our family!” Silambayi warned him.

“All right, Amma! I will go and receive the blessing of merely seeing the faces of my aunt and uncle. Let my sisters come with me too, Amma. I think they too will wish to see them!” Vaiyam Perumaan said, looking at Muthayi and Pavalayi.

“All right, Amma! You too go with your brother and return immediately,” Silambayi said, giving them leave.

In the festival pavilion erected at the temple shrine, Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchi were wholeheartedly occupied, along with their people, in lighting *maavilakku* for the Amman.

Kundrudaiyaan looked somewhat older, but the radiance of his face had not changed at all. Thamarai's brown complexion had darkened from the unceasing agricultural labour she had shared with her husband, yet the grace of that face remained just as it had been. Both appeared energetic. Near them stood a man with gold chains around his neck, bracelets on his arms and rings shining on all ten fingers. He was

<!-- source boundary: scan 105 → scan 106 -->

Thanakkodi Chettiar of Aadhisettip Paalayam. He too was worshipping the Amman alongside Pavalayi.

Vaiyam Perumaan, Muthayi and Pavalayi came and stood near them. Hearing the greeting “Vanakkam,” Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai Naachchi turned and looked. The moment she saw them, Thamarai Naachchi's heart began pounding. Her eyes grew moist. Her fingers trembled. Her whole body shook.

She knew at once: these were the children of her elder brother Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu.

Could it be surprising that she had heard what everyone who came to the karagam festival had heard?

She too had learned that Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's daughters had become trapped in the Kaveri while releasing the karagam and that two brave young men had saved them and brought them ashore. Yet twenty-five years of enmity between the two families had grown, taken root and spread. So Thamarai Naachchiyar had heard it merely as news, had been startled and then relieved; she had not suffered any particular anguish.

But now her elder brother's children were standing before her. A feeling of kinship and affection rose up, trampling over every sense of enmity and taking possession of her.

On her wedding day, her brother had watched as she and her husband were driven out. Yet even the molten fire of that volcano of pain was being extinguished now by a rain of ice called affection.

Seeing Thamarai Naachchiyar's state, Kundrudaiyaan shook her shoulder.

“Do not give way to emotion, Thamarai! You already have pain in your chest. Even two days ago you suffered from it.

<!-- source boundary: scan 106 → scan 107 -->

Do not forget that. Take everything calmly,” he said.

But Thamarai Naachchiyar forgot herself. She embraced Muthayi, Pavalayi and Vaiyam Perumaan, breathed in the crowns of their heads affectionately and kissed them.

A great rain poured from her eyes. The girls and Vaiyam Perumaan too grew heavy-hearted and unsteady. In the joyous dance of family affection, pages from many years of life were turning. Without words, their hearts asked after one another's welfare. Kundrudaiyaan embraced Vaiyam Perumaan and kissed Muthayi and Pavalayi on the crowns of their heads. Then he looked at Thanakkodi Chettiar, who stood motionless beside him watching everything, and said, “Chettiar Ayya! These are Chinnamalaik Kozhundhu's treasures—my nieces and my nephew!”

“Oh, is that so?... If only your sons had been alive, they would have tied the *thaali* for these girls and fulfilled Thamarai Naachchiyammai's vow,” Thanakkodi Chettiar said in teasing words mixed with pain.

“Do not speak of all that now. Does everything happen as we imagine?” Kundrudaiyaan sighed deeply in distress.

Thamarai Naachchiyar did her best to stop her eyes from becoming waterfalls once again.

Looking at Vaiyam Perumaan, Muthayi and Pavalayi, she said, “All right—you should go now. If your mother and father see you, it will only create needless trouble.”

“We are going, Aththai! We came to find out one thing. If you, Mama or Chettiar know, please tell us,” Vaiyam Perumaan said.

“What is it?” Kundrudaiyaan interrupted.

“Amma wishes to perform an *archana* to the Amman so that the young men who saved my sisters may live well. But we do not know their names. We came to ask whether you know them,” Vaiyam Perumaan replied.

“We too asked around—who are those young men? No one seems to know!” Kundrudaiyaan said.
