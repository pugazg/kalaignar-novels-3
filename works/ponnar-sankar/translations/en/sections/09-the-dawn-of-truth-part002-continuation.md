The incident that had taken place twenty-five years earlier seemed to trouble Maayavar's mind even now, for his eyes appeared slightly moist.

“Why did that happiness not last?” Raakkiyannan asked with great eagerness.

“Even before I had returned from Maniyangurichi, at Chellaatha Gounder's instigation Thalaiyur Kaali had written a threatening letter to Malaikkozhundha Gounder. The result of that letter was that Nelliyangodan and Thamarai Naachchi were immediately driven out of the Maniyangurichi mansion. It was the very day of their wedding—even the rites that should have followed the marriage ceremony had not been properly carried through to completion. They gave Thamarai Naachchiyar all the ceremonial gifts of gold and silver and then drove that golden Thamarai out in a flood of tears, telling her that neither she nor her husband was permitted to set foot in that house again—as though serving a six-flavoured feast and saying, ‘I have mixed only an atom of poison into it; drink it happily.’ Nelliyangodan understood nothing. He sank into worry over how they were to live. Thamarai, who valued family honour and culture, was a woman with the resolve that she and her husband must somehow live and with a sense of the right to struggle for it. So, together with her husband, she went to Chellaatha Gounder to argue that, even if he was unwilling to hand over the rule that belonged to her husband, he should at least give them a portion of that territory. Do you know what happened at Chellaatha Gounder's mansion?”

Tears hung in Maayavar's eyes.

There was silence for a little while. Within that training camp, the two of them now seemed to contain the whole world. Though Raakkiyannan did not show his agitation very openly, he was eager to know what had happened.

“Chellaatha Gounder and Maanthiyappan acted very well as though they were honouring their visitors. They said Thamarai could remain

at the mansion while Nelliyangodan was taken out so that the boundary of the land he needed could be fixed. Deceiving him in this way, Chellaatha Gounder took Nelliyangodan alone in his carriage!”

“And then? Then?” Raakkiyannan waited anxiously for Maayavar's tongue to move.

“What else? They dragged Nelliyangodan to the edge of a field, tied him upside down to a tree and tortured him, beating his whole body raw with karuvelam switches. Back at the mansion, Maanthiyappan even dared to misbehave with Thamarai.”

Maayavar had not finished speaking when Raakkiyannan cried out, “Ayyo!”

“Thamarai Naachchi is not merely a queen of chastity; she has the courage and firmness of Kannagi Devi! She kicked Maanthiyappan and sent him sprawling. Maanthiyappan, who had bitten the dust, flew into a terrible rage, drew his sword and tried to cut off her hair.”

“What?” Raakkiyannan's moustache quivered as he asked.

“But Thamarai snatched the sword from his hand and ran in search of her husband. She swung the sword at the scoundrels who were beating him with karuvelam switches. Those villains fled from the place, glad simply to have escaped alive. Thamarai left Valanaadu with the sword raised in one hand and the other arm around the golden body of her wounded husband. When I heard of this two days later, I immediately met Kaali and asked him in anguish, ‘Can you too stand by while Chellaatha Gounder commits such atrocities?’ Kaali said he knew nothing of it and told me that if such an incident had indeed taken place, it

deserved condemnation. I told him it was not enough to say that to me—he had to convey his condemnation to Chellaatha Gounder and his son Maanthiyappan as well. He nodded as if in agreement. I do not know whether he sent any message condemning them. But before long, when Chellaatha Gounder and Maanthiyappan committed another injustice against Nelliyangodan, Thalaiyur Kaali sent his own men to help and won his patron's smile as his reward.”

“What was that next injustice?” At Raakkiyannan's question, Maayavar resumed his answer.

“Nelliyangodan and Thamarai Naachchi went to the Chola king at Uraiyur. They reminded him that Kolaatha Gounder had served under the Chola king as the guardian-ruler of Valanaadu and that Nelliyangodan was Kolaatha Gounder's son, and they asked for help. They also explained how Chellaatha Gounder, with Thalaiyur Kaali's support, had stripped them of their rights. The Chola was moved by their sorrowful story. But he did not wish to invade Valanaadu on their behalf and thereby seek Thalaiyur Kaali's enmity. Even so, regarding aid to them as a way of showing gratitude to Kolaatha Gounder, he gave them a great extent of fallow land, ploughing cattle and agricultural implements.”

“Long live the Chola!” Raakkiyannan's lips exclaimed. Maayavar too said, “Yes! Long live! Long live!” Raakkiyannan watched closely to hear what he would say next.

“Nelliyangodan, who ought to have held a sceptre and exercised authority, began instead to hold a plough and farm. Employing the workers they needed, husband and wife laboured hard in wet and dry fields alike. As the saying goes that labour brings advancement, the crops they planted flourished luxuriantly. Thamarai and Nelliyangodan delighted in looking at their land, which pleased the eye like a green carpet

spread before them. When Chellaatha Gounder and Maanthiyappan heard of this, their faces tightened. At once a palm-leaf message flew to Thalaiyur Kaali. I was beside Thalaiyur Kaali when that message arrived!”

“What message was it, Maayavar?” Raakkiyannan's eagerness increased.

“Hundreds of Thalaiyur Kaali's soldiers were to come at once. They were to drive herds of wild boar before them and ruin every field Nelliyangodan had cultivated. That was the message Chellaatha Gounder had sent. I tried to stop it, saying it was wrong and should not be done. Thalaiyur Kaali too hesitated at first. He was confused, asking, ‘How can I disregard Chellaatha Gounder's request?’ I opposed it stubbornly. He would not listen. Kaali said, ‘When my father was dying, he told me to act according to Chellaatha Gounder's wishes. So I cannot refuse his word.’ He sent the soldiers and the herds of boar to destroy the crops. Great farming people may even endure lives being taken—but how can they endure crops being destroyed? That day I could bear it no longer. I left Thalaiyur. It took twenty-five years for the wound in my heart to heal.” Maayavar sighed deeply, then looked at Raakkiyannan and asked, “Yes—where are Nelliyangodan and his wife Thamarai now? I heard that they live at Kudaiyur on the southern bank of the Amaravathi. Is that true? Are they alive?”

“Not merely alive—they are thriving! The property Nelliyangodan and Thamarai earned through their labour has grown. Nelliyangodan has come to own many tracts of land surrounded by hills, and he is now known by the title Kundrudaiyaan,” Raakkiyannan said.

“I was told even that. I am glad! But one thing—according to the vow Thamarai Naachchiyar made to her brother, they have no sons at all, do they? I heard that they have only one daughter. Am I right?” Maayavar asked.

Raakkiyannan now laughed. He looked intently at Maayavar.

“You are not only a true friend and a good man; the time for truths to come out has now blossomed as well, so I shall tell you. The two sons born to Kundrudaiyaan and Thamarai—Ponnar and Sankar—are receiving martial training from me right here in this camp!”

“Is that so?”

“You must have seen on your way here those brave young men who struck down several boars?”

“The eldest of the three is Veeramalai Sambuvan, son of Chozhan Thotti. Of the other two, the elder is Ponnar and the younger is Sankar!”
