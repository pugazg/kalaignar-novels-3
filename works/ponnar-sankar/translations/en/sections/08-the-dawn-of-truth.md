7

The Dawn of Truth

“What is this, Raakkiyannan? Is your explosive laughter the answer to my question?” Maayavar pressed him.

“Nothing like that. I do not reveal to anyone the names of the students who receive martial training from me. I have issued strict orders that, whoever asks, those students too must not give their names,” Raakkiyannan replied firmly.

Maayavar would not let it go. “There was never such an order before for students training in this camp! When Prince Kaali of Thalaiyur was your student and received martial training from you, no student's name here was kept secret,” he said in surprise.

Clearing his throat loudly, Raakkiyannan rose from his chair and paced the training camp with the dignity of a *vengai*. “Maayavar! What you are talking about belongs to thirty years ago. Who could fail to know Prince Kaali, son of King Periya Kaali of Thalaiyur? When he was young and studied here, learning methods of war such as archery, elephant riding and horse riding, there was no need to hide his name. Nor could it have been hidden. The situation is not like that now. Because revealing the names of a few of the students I have today would not be good for

<!-- source boundary: scan 70 → scan 71 -->

those students themselves, a new rule has been in force in this camp for some years: no student is to give his name to anyone who asks.” After explaining this, he turned to Maayavar with a new question.

“Yes—you left the Thalaiyur palace about twenty or twenty-five years ago after announcing that you were going away as an ascetic. I heard that you returned to Thalaiyur again a year or two ago. Once, while I was swimming in the Amaravathi, I even saw you riding quickly along the riverbank. I thought of calling out to you, but at the speed you were travelling my voice would not have reached your ears, so I remained silent. After renouncing everything and wandering to so many places, what made you renounce renunciation itself and return?”

Maayavar answered this question with a smile.

“Only if I had actually embraced asceticism could I renounce it, could I not? When Prince Kaali assumed responsibility for the Thalaiyur rule after King Periya Kaali, I myself was appointed minister and looked after all the affairs of government. Nothing in Thalaiyur took place against my advice. But that situation did not last long. I realised that circumstances were arising in which my words had no value before Thalaiyur Kaali. My conscience began to trouble me: must I too bear responsibility for the wrongs that were taking place? Unwilling to remain in office under that distress, I told Kaali himself and left the palace. For more than ten years I travelled through many regions—Chola country, Chera country and Pandya country. Later I went north as far as Kasi. I wandered through cities and small settlements along the Ganga and Yamuna. I stayed for many years in Buddhist *viharas* and Jain *pallis* and studied many great books. In this way twenty-five

<!-- source boundary: scan 71 → scan 72 -->

years passed, yet all my thoughts kept circling around Tamil Nadu. Above all, I worried about Thalaiyur Kaali. We had left him alone for twenty-five years; when his father died, had he not entrusted the boy to me and gone after telling me to stand by him? Another worry gripped me—that I had cast aside that responsibility. In short, attachment to the land of my birth seized me and pulled me back.”

Maayavar's answer sank deeply and movingly into Raakkiyannan's mind.

“What wound arose in your heart that made you leave a king of Thalaiyur for whom you had such attachment and affection? I suppose time may have healed that wound. If you think it right, could you not give me the opportunity to learn of it too?”

“That day I left the place without even telling you, my close friend. As atonement for that, I have no hesitation in telling you the reason now.”

“Perhaps you feared that if you had come to take leave of me then, I might have stopped you. Whatever happened has happened. At least from now on, may your knowledge, ability and pure heart be of use so that what happens next will be good.”

“Raakkiyannar! You think of what is good. But even now I do not have that confidence in the Thalaiyur palace.”

“Is that so? Why, Maayavar? What makes you say this?”

“Even now, is not Thalaiyur Kaali still enchanted by the flattering words of his beloved patron Chellaatha Gounder? Is it not the web Chellaatha Gounder spins

<!-- source boundary: scan 72 → scan 73 -->

that ensnares Thalaiyur Kaali? Raakkiyannar! Chellaatha Gounder won King Periya Kaali over through his skill with words, took the prince, who was then a boy, to Valanaadu itself and raised him there. It was to repay that debt of gratitude that Periya Kaali helped Chellaatha Gounder in many ways. Kolaatha Gounder left Vaazhavanthi Seemai, settled in Thirukkaampuliyur, restored the Amman temple at Vaangal and conducted its *kudamuzhukku* festival. You too knew in your youth that Kolaatha Gounder stood as the minor ruler over the region containing villages such as Maarikkavundan Paalayam, Pasupathipaalayam, Kuppachchi Paalayam, Panchamaadevi, Karuppam Paalayam, Kadambankurichi and Vaangal. With Sirraalai Pattinam as his headquarters, he ruled the region called Nelli Valanaadu. One of the lakes he created was Vellaangulam Lake. Because he ruled by giving priority to agriculture, it earned the praise, ‘Nelli Valanaadu, the *Konaadu* encircled by paddy.’ Nelliyangodan was the son born to end the long-standing sorrow of his parents. Yet the boy born to satisfy that longing became a cause of great worry to them. He grew up without understanding the world and became one to whom the label *masai* applied. He was the sort who might ask what price one paid for briskness and energy. He was without deceit or guile. Worried about how he could ever administer Nelli Valanaadu, Kolaatha Gounder entrusted him to his brother Chellaatha Gounder, asked that he be raised under his protection, and died. After his father Kolaatha Gounder, his mother Pavalaathaal too died, and Nelliyangodan became an orphan. Chellaatha Gounder then planned to seize the entire authority over Valanaadu that belonged to him. King Periya Kaali of Thalaiyur decided that if Valanaadu came under the responsibility of Nelliyangodan, who was a ‘Machchasaami,’ it would not remain a strong region, and that if a powerful administration capable of supporting Thalaiyur rule was to exist in Nelli Valanaadu, Chellaatha Gounder's scheming mind was the suitable one.

<!-- source boundary: scan 73 → scan 74 -->

To carry out that decision, Chellaatha Gounder explained his plans to Periya Kaali. On Chellaatha Gounder's advice, Nelliyangodan was driven out of Valanaadu while still young. Whenever he returned and argued with Chellaatha Gounder that his lands should be handed back to him, he was subjected to severe torture. In all this Chellaatha Gounder was able to obtain Thalaiyur's help. When Periya Kaali lay on his deathbed, I, Chellaatha Gounder, Prince Kaali and several other chief officers of the state were beside him. King Periya Kaali said that he was appointing me as minister to stand by Prince Kaali and look after the burden of government. Then he placed Prince Kaali's hand in Chellaatha Gounder's and said, ‘Gounder Ayya! From now on you are both mother and father to my son Kaali.’ Turning to the prince he said, ‘Thambi Kaali! Chellaatha Gounder is your patron. The relationship between his Valanaadu and our Menaadu is one that must continue forever. Do not answer his word with another word. Take what he says as my own word and act accordingly.’ With that, he died.”

Before Maayavar had finished giving these details, Raakkiyannan interrupted. “Ade paavi! Even after knowing Chellaatha Gounder for so long, did he not understand his nature?” he said, expressing his distress.

Maayavar continued with further details.

“After he was orphaned, Nelliyangodan went once or twice to Aarichampatti-Maniyangurichi, secretly met Thamarai Naachchi and somehow managed to live on with some help from her. When the man of Maniyangurichi learned that affection had arisen between them, he drove Nelliyangodan away so that he would not come to that side again, fearing that the affection might ripen fully. Chellaatha Gounder learned of this. Could he have been unaware of the close family relationship between Maniyangurichi Malaikkozhundha Gounder and Nelliyangodan?

<!-- source boundary: scan 74 → scan 75 -->

So, both to prevent such a relationship from becoming stronger and to bring the strength of the major landholder of Aarichampatti-Maniyangurichi to his own side, he planned to make Thamarai Naachchi the wife of his son Maanthiyappan and succeeded in those marriage negotiations.

Raakkiyannan interrupted with a curling smile. “Maayavar! But that marriage did not take place as Chellaatha Gounder wished, did it? It took place according to Thamarai Naachchiyar's wish!”

“It was because I disliked the disastrous events that followed that I had to leave Thalaiyur,” Maayavar said.

“You still have not even reached the point of telling me the reason why you became angry with Thalaiyur Kaali twenty or twenty-five years ago and left for other places!” Raakkiyannan said teasingly.

“I have reached it! I have now returned to Thalaiyur, and I have also reached the reason you want to know,” Maayavar said, stroking his beard and casting a cool look at Raakkiyannan.

“When the Thamarai Naachchi-Maanthiyappan wedding was stopped, Thalaiyur Kaali returned to the palace the next day. I met him and praised him. I told him: ‘You, who are always the first to stand by Chellaatha Gounder without crossing the line he draws, have in this one matter saved a woman's life from being ruined.’ I praised him greatly. He did not treat me merely as a minister serving beneath him. Because I was older than he was, he touched my feet in respect and thanked me for the praise. Thalaiyur Kaali has always shown me that kind of respect and regard. I was happy, thinking, ‘The boy is slowly freeing himself from Chellaatha Gounder's spiderweb.’ But that happiness did not last!”
